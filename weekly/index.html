Jobs · Canada

Canada sheds jobs. Unemployment hits a six-month high.

Economists expected 13,600 new jobs. Instead, 4,700 disappeared, one week after the Bank of Canada raised rates.

Economy Desk · November 6, 2026 · Source: Statistics Canada
6.7%
Unemployment, October
−4,700
Jobs lost in October
2.50%
Bank of Canada rate, up from 2.25%

Private employers cut 52,500 jobs in October. New public-sector jobs (49,600) covered most of it. Unemployment for 15 to 24-year-olds climbed to 13.7%, while workers aged 25 to 54 held steady. Job losses for 2026 so far add up to about 46,000.

The timing is awkward. On October 28 the Bank of Canada made its first rate hike since 2023 to stop high gas prices spreading into everything else. Its next decision is December 9.

SCAM WATCH

“This is the RCMP.” No, it isn’t.

Scammers are faking real police numbers on your caller ID. RCMP warned about it across Canada on Oct 22, and again in Alberta on Nov 6.

STEP 1Hang up.

You don’t need a reason. Real police can wait.

STEP 2Look up.

Find the number yourself, on the official website. Never use one the caller gives you.

STEP 3Call back.

Ask if the call was real. For your bank, use the number on your card.

Read the full guide: what to do, step by step →
How the call works

Five moves, every time

  1. Your screen shows a real number, like your local RCMP detachment. It’s spoofed.
  2. The caller sounds official, with a rank and a fake badge number.
  3. You are told you’re the victim of a financial crime.
  4. Then the ask: bank details, a “safe” account, or a courier for your card.
  5. And the secret: “Don’t tell anyone, not even your bank.”
Real police never

One of these = scam

  • Ask for money over the phone
  • Ask for gift cards, crypto or money transfers
  • Ask for your card details or send someone to collect it
  • Ask you to move money to a “safe” account
  • Ask you to keep an investigation secret
Say this: “I’ll call the detachment back on its public number.” Then hang up.
Already paid or shared details?

Call your bank first. Then report it.

Freeze your cards and stop transfers using the number on your card. Then report to local police and the Canadian Anti-Fraud Centre at 1-888-495-8501. Keep call times, numbers and screenshots.
