Rogers Fined $15 Million Over “Unlimited” Ads. What It Means for You

Published November 6, 2026 · Updated November 6, 2026 · 4 min read

Short answer: on October 28, 2026, the Competition Tribunal ordered Rogers to pay a $15 million penalty over how it marketed its Infinite plans. The penalty goes to the government, and no refund process for customers has been announced. If your plan isn’t what you were told, complain to your provider first, then the free CCTS.

$15M
Penalty ordered against Rogers by the Competition Tribunal, Oct 28, 2026

The Competition Bureau took Rogers to the Tribunal over ads for its “Infinite” and unlimited wireless plans. The Tribunal sided with the Bureau. Rogers says it stands behind its advertising. A fine judges the advertiser; it does not read your contract for you, so read your own terms. This is part of The Weekly Economy, our weekly Canadian money brief.

What the Tribunal Decided

  • A $15 million penalty against Rogers.
  • A corrective notice: Rogers must publish one.
  • The Bureau’s legal costs: Rogers must pay them.
  • No refunds for customers. The Bureau had asked for a $15 million penalty with customer refunds, or a larger penalty without them. The Tribunal chose the penalty without refunds.

The Tribunal’s full reasons were still being redacted when this was written, with a public version expected within about 10 business days. They may add detail.

What the Bureau Said About “Unlimited”

The Competition Bureau argued that the plans were marketed as unrestricted, even though speeds dropped sharply after a set amount of data. Reports describe the slowdown as more than 99%.

Rogers’ position: it denies making any false or misleading claim. It says customers get enough full-speed data that the plans are effectively unlimited for normal use. A challenge to the constitutionality of the law used was dismissed. Rogers says it is “considering next steps”, which may mean an appeal.

Check Your Own Plan

  1. Find your plan’s terms in your online account or contract: how much data you get at full speed.
  2. Find the speed after that: what your speed drops to, and whether it is stated in plain numbers.
  3. Compare with the ad or offer you signed up under. Screenshot it if it is still online.
  4. Test it: if your speed feels far slower than you were told, note the dates and times.

The same check works for any plan advertised as “unlimited”, from any provider.

If Your Plan Falls Short

  1. Write to your provider. Quote the ad or offer, explain the gap, and say what you want: a fix, a better plan, or a credit. Ask for a reference number.
  2. Escalate to its customer relations team if the first answer doesn’t help.
  3. Go to the CCTS, free, at 1-888-221-1687, if the provider doesn’t resolve it.
  4. You can also report misleading advertising to the Competition Bureau.

Frequently Asked

Do I get money from the $15 million Rogers penalty?

No claims process has been announced. The Tribunal ordered a penalty without customer refunds, and a penalty of this kind is paid to the government, not to customers.

Did Rogers admit it did anything wrong?

No. Rogers says it stands behind its advertising and is considering next steps.

Is my Rogers plan really unlimited?

Check your plan terms. The Bureau argued that speeds fell sharply after a set amount of data. Look for the full-speed data amount and the speed after it.

Does this ruling apply to Bell, Telus or other providers?

The ruling is about Rogers’ Infinite plans. The same check works for any plan advertised as unlimited.

Where do I complain about my phone plan in Canada?

Start with your provider in writing. If it doesn’t resolve the problem, the Commission for Complaints for Telecom-television Services (CCTS) handles complaints for free at 1-888-221-1687.
