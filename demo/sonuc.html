Canadian Client Won't Pay Your Invoice? Here's the Path

Test page · Roadmap draft · 2 min read

Short answer: a US business can recover money from a Canadian company in 7 steps: tell us the case, check the company, check your deadline, gather proof, send a demand letter, choose a route, then enforce. Start cheap and escalate only if needed.

The Roadmap
→Your Case →Check Company →Deadline →Proof →Demand Letter →Your Route →Get Paid →Start Now →FAQ

1. Tell us your case

1Who owes you? Company name and the province it is in.

2How much? The unpaid amount and the invoice dates.

3What did you agree? A signed contract, a purchase order, or just emails.

↑ Back to sections

2. Check the company

1Is it real and active? Search the federal or provincial corporate registry for its legal name and status.

2Is it bankrupt or insolvent? Search the federal bankruptcy records (OSB). If it is, you don't sue: you file a proof of claim with the trustee.

↑ Back to sections

3. Check your deadline

Waiting can cost you the claim. In Ontario, the general deadline to sue is 2 years, and enforcing a US judgment usually has a 2-year deadline too. Other provinces have their own rules.

↑ Back to sections

4. Gather proof

Contract, invoices, emails, delivery records and any payment promises. Check the contract for an arbitration or jurisdiction clause: it can decide where the dispute must be heard.

5. Send a demand letter

A formal letter with the amount owed, a clear deadline, and what you will do next. Many disputes end here, before any fees.

Frequently asked

Do I need a lawyer?

Not for small claims court, which is built for people without lawyers. For large amounts or superior court, a lawyer is usually worth it.

How long do I have?

In Ontario, the general deadline to sue is 2 years. Other provinces set their own deadlines, so check early.

What if the company went bankrupt?

You usually can't sue. Instead, file a proof of claim with the licensed insolvency trustee handling the case.

Can I sue in a US court instead?

Yes, but to collect from Canadian assets you then need a Canadian court to recognize the judgment. In Ontario that usually must start within 2 years.
