Interest rates · Bank of Canada

The Bank of Canada says it may hike again. The loonie hits a 19-month low.

The Bank’s notes from its October meeting came out on Nov 10: more increases are possible, one meeting at a time. Markets now put a December hike at about 60 to 66%. The dollar slid to about 70 U.S. cents.

The summary shows the council weighed holding at 2.25% against raising to 2.50%, and raised. Members agreed there could be a need to increase the rate further, and said they will decide one meeting at a time. Their worry is energy: the longer energy prices stay high, the greater the risk that they spread to other prices.

The Bank now expects inflation to reach 3.5% at the end of this year and to stay above 3% for much of 2027. Businesses have mostly not passed higher costs on to shoppers so far, but the Bank’s surveys suggest they expect to raise some prices over the next year. October’s inflation number is due Monday, Nov 16. Economists expect about 3.2%, after 3.1% in September.

What it means for you

Variable rate or line of credit? Get your number before December 9.

Prime is 4.70% and moves with the Bank’s rate. Ask your lender now what a quarter-point rise would add to your payment and whether you can lock in. Renewal coming up? Ask for the offer in writing. If the bank gets it wrong, you can complain for free.

SCAM WATCH

A “free phone” from your carrier? Check first.

Edmonton police say callers posing as Rogers, Telus and Virgin Mobile offered free or heavily discounted phones, had people order devices through their real providers, then collected and resold them. Police link the network to more than $20 million in losses and to call centres in Lahore, Pakistan. Several have been shut down and one man is charged.

STEP 1Don’t decide on the call.

A real offer will still be there when you call back yourself.

STEP 2Call your carrier.

Use the number on your bill or the carrier’s app, not one the caller gives you.

STEP 3Keep the phone.

Never ship or hand a new device to anyone because a caller told you to.

How to report a scam in Canada, step by step →
How the call works

Four moves

  1. A call from “your carrier” with a free or heavily discounted phone.
  2. You are asked for personal or account details.
  3. You are told to order a new device through your real provider.
  4. The device is collected from you and resold.
Caller ID can lie

Red flags this week

  • A caller ID that shows your carrier, your bank or the police
  • A deal that is only good if you say yes on this call
  • A request to send a new phone somewhere else
  • A text asking for a small fee to release a parcel
  • Payment by gift card, crypto or Bitcoin machine
Say this: “I’ll call the number on my bill and ask about it.” Then hang up.
Already ordered the phone?

Call your carrier today. Then report it.

Call your provider on the number on your bill, say you were targeted by a phone-deal scam and ask it to flag the order and your account. Report to local police and the Canadian Anti-Fraud Centre at 1-888-495-8501. If the provider will not fix the charges, the CCTS takes complaints for free.
