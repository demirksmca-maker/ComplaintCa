Complain About an Electricity or Gas Bill

Last updated: July 27, 2026 · Reviewed against official sources

Billing errors, unexplained rate increases, or a service issue with your power or gas utility — every province has an independent energy regulator, but you have to go through your utility's own complaints process first.

Required First Step: Your Utility's Own Process

Every regulator below expects you to have already filed a complaint directly with your electricity or gas provider and given them a chance to respond. Keep your account number, the dates you contacted them, and their response — you'll need it to escalate.

Energy Regulators By Province

Ontario

Ontario Energy Board (OEB) — oeb.ca/consumer-protection
Covers electricity and natural gas utilities, sub-meter providers, and energy retailers.

British Columbia

BC Utilities Commission (BCUC) — bcuc.com/WhatWeDo/ComplaintProcess

Alberta

Alberta Utilities Commission (AUC) — auc.ab.ca/enforcement-make-complaint

Quebec

Régie de l'énergie — regie-energie.qc.ca
File with Hydro-Québec's complaints office first; an appeal to the Régie carries a review fee, and its decision is final.

Manitoba

Manitoba Public Utilities Board (PUB) — pubmanitoba.ca

Saskatchewan and other provinces

Saskatchewan's power and gas utilities (SaskPower, SaskEnergy) are Crown corporations without a separate independent complaints commission in the same structure as OEB or BCUC — if their own complaints process doesn't resolve things, Ombudsman Saskatchewan can review how the Crown corporation handled your complaint. Frequently Asked

Who do I complain to about my electricity bill in Ontario?

Your utility first, then the Ontario Energy Board if unresolved.

Who regulates utility complaints in BC?

The BC Utilities Commission, after you've tried resolving it with the utility directly.

Who regulates utility complaints in Alberta?

The Alberta Utilities Commission, including unfair or incorrect rate charges.

How do I complain about Hydro-Québec?

File with Hydro-Québec's complaints office first, then appeal to the Régie de l'énergie, whose decision is final.

Do I have to contact my utility before the regulator?

Yes, in every province — regulators expect proof you tried resolving it directly first.
