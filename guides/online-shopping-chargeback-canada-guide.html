1. Contact the Merchant First, in Writing

Email or message the seller describing the problem and what you want (refund, replacement, or partial credit). Keep the message — most card issuers ask for proof you tried this before they'll open a chargeback.

2. Request a Chargeback From Your Card Issuer

Call the number on the back of your card and ask specifically for a "chargeback" or "dispute" for goods not received or significantly not as described. Do this within roughly 90–120 days of the transaction — the exact window depends on your card network and issuer, so don't wait.

Paid by Interac Debit? There's no equivalent formal chargeback system — go to step 3 instead.

3. File With Your Provincial Consumer Protection Office

If the merchant won't cooperate and a chargeback isn't available or was denied, your province's consumer protection office can investigate the business, especially if it's Canadian-based.

Quick Answer

Write to the seller first and keep the message. Then call your card issuer and ask for a chargeback for goods not received or not as described, within about 90–120 days of the purchase. Paid by Interac Debit, or chargeback denied? File with your provincial consumer protection office, and use small claims court for larger amounts.

Paid Another Way?

  • Credit card — ask your card issuer for a chargeback, within about 90–120 days.
  • PayPal — open a dispute in the PayPal Resolution Centre. Buyer Protection generally allows 180 days from the payment.
  • Interac e-Transfer — call your bank right away. It may be cancelled if not yet deposited; after that it usually can't be reversed.
  • Interac Debit — no formal chargeback. Use your provincial consumer office or small claims court.
  • Buy now, pay later (Affirm, Afterpay, Klarna) — open a dispute in the app; payments may be paused while it's reviewed.
  • Gift cards or crypto — very hard to recover. Contact the card company or exchange at once, then report to the CAFC.

Where to Report an Online Store for Scamming

Getting your money back and reporting the store are two separate steps — do both. Report the store to the Canadian Anti-Fraud Centre (1-888-495-8501), your provincial consumer protection office (list in step 3 above), and the Competition Bureau if the ads were fake or misleading. If you found the seller on a marketplace or social media, report the listing there too. For the full reporting order, see the scam and fraud reporting guide.

Frequently Asked

What is a chargeback?

A reversal of a credit card charge requested through your card issuer, used when goods never arrived or weren't as described.

How long do I have to file a chargeback?

Often 90–120 days from the transaction, depending on your card network and issuer — don't wait to find out.

Does debit have chargeback protection too?

Interac Debit doesn't have the same formal system. Contact the merchant, your provincial consumer office, or consider small claims for larger amounts.

Should I contact the merchant or my bank first?

The merchant first, in writing — most issuers require proof you tried to resolve it directly.

What if it's a scam, not just a bad experience?

Also report it to the Canadian Anti-Fraud Centre at 1-888-495-8501, in addition to requesting a chargeback. See ComplaintCA's guide on how to report a scam or fraud in Canada for the complete reporting order.

I paid with PayPal — how do I get my money back?

Open a dispute in the PayPal Resolution Centre. PayPal Buyer Protection generally gives you 180 days from the payment date for items not received or significantly not as described.

I paid by Interac e-Transfer — can I get it back?

Call your bank right away. If the seller hasn't deposited it yet, the bank may be able to cancel it. Once deposited, an e-Transfer usually can't be reversed, so report the scam to the CAFC and your provincial consumer office.

Where do I report an online store for scamming?

Report it to the Canadian Anti-Fraud Centre (1-888-495-8501), your provincial consumer protection office, and the Competition Bureau for fake or misleading ads. Report it to the marketplace or platform where you found it too.

What is ComplaintCA?

A Canadian civic platform that drafts complaints and chargeback requests with AI in 7 languages and identifies the right provincial office for unresolved disputes.

I'm Deaf or hard of hearing — how do I call a phone-only authority?

Use Canada VRS (free sign-language video relay, available 24/7) or dial 711 for a text-based relay operator — both let you place a real phone call to any number listed on this page.
