If Money or Banking Details Were Already Shared

Contact your bank or credit card issuer immediately — ask about reversing the transaction, freezing the card, or flagging the account. Minutes matter here.

Canadian Anti-Fraud Centre (CAFC)

The national body for tracking fraud and cyber crime intelligence across Canada. It does not investigate individual cases, but reporting is essential for police and regulators to act on patterns.

Report at antifraudcentre-centreantifraude.ca — 1-888-495-8501 →

Note: the CAFC does not accept reports by email — use the phone line or its online Fraud Reporting System.

Your Local Police Service

File a report with your local police (non-emergency line) — this creates an official record, which some banks and insurers require to process a claim.

Quick Answer

Scammed online? Stop paying and stop replying. Call your bank or card issuer first — the faster you call, the better your chance of getting money back. Then report it to the Canadian Anti-Fraud Centre at 1-888-495-8501 and to your local police, and keep every message and receipt.

Scammed Online? What to Do in the First 24 Hours

  1. Stop contact and payments. Don't send "fees" to unlock a refund — that is a second scam.
  2. Call your bank or card issuer using the number on the back of your card. See how to report fraud to your bank (RBC, TD, CIBC).
  3. Secure your accounts. Change passwords, turn on two-step login, and remove any remote-access app the scammer asked you to install.
  4. Save evidence. Screenshots, emails, website address, receipts and transaction numbers.
  5. Report it to the CAFC and your local police, and get a police file number.

How to Get Your Money Back, by Payment Type

  • Credit card — ask for a chargeback, usually within 90–120 days. See the chargeback guide.
  • PayPal — open a dispute in the Resolution Centre; Buyer Protection generally allows 180 days.
  • Interac e-Transfer — call your bank at once. It may be cancelled if not yet deposited; after that it usually can't be reversed.
  • Debit or bank transfer — report to your bank's fraud team right away and ask for a fraud claim.
  • Gift cards or crypto — very hard to recover. Contact the gift card company or exchange immediately, then report to the CAFC.

Common Cyber Crime Types

  • Phishing — fake emails/texts impersonating banks, government, or delivery services asking for credentials or payment.
  • Identity theft — someone using your personal information to open accounts or make purchases.
  • Romance & investment scams — long-term manipulation building trust before requesting money or crypto.
  • Online harassment & extortion — threats, doxxing, or sextortion attempts.

Frequently Asked

How do I report a scam or fraud in Canada?

Contact the Canadian Anti-Fraud Centre at 1-888-495-8501 or reportcyberandfraud.canada.ca, and file a report with your local police service.

Does the CAFC accept reports by email?

No — use the phone line or the online Fraud Reporting System only.

How can I check if a message is a phishing scam?

Upload it to ComplaintCA's document verification tool for an AI analysis, done on your device.

I already sent money — what do I do first?

Contact your bank or card issuer immediately, then report to the CAFC and your local police.

How do I report a company that has committed online fraud?

Report the company to the Canadian Anti-Fraud Centre at 1-888-495-8501, and if you paid by card, dispute the charge with your card issuer directly.

How do I report online shopping fraud in Canada?

Dispute the charge with your card issuer, keep your order confirmation and seller messages, and report the incident to the CAFC. See ComplaintCA's online shopping chargeback guide for exact deadlines.

How do I report a bank scam or a fake bank text?

Call your bank using the number on the back of your card — never a number or link from the message — then report the message to the CAFC.

How do I report online blackmail or extortion?

Don't pay or respond. Save all messages as evidence, then report it to your local police and the CAFC.

What should I do if I was scammed online?

Stop all contact and payments, call your bank or card issuer right away, change your passwords, save every message and receipt, then report it to the Canadian Anti-Fraud Centre and your local police.

Can I get my money back after being scammed online in Canada?

It depends on how you paid. Credit card payments can often be disputed with a chargeback, PayPal has Buyer Protection disputes, and an Interac e-Transfer may be cancelled only if it hasn't been deposited yet. Gift cards and crypto are very hard to recover. Act within hours, not days.

I sent an Interac e-Transfer to a scammer — what now?

Call your bank immediately and ask them to stop or cancel the transfer. If it was already deposited, it usually can't be reversed, but report it to your bank's fraud team, the CAFC and police — it helps if the account is frozen later.

What is ComplaintCA?

A Canadian civic complaint platform that routes you to the correct authority, with AI-assisted drafting in 7 languages, an MP finder, a lawyer panel, and cyber crime reporting support.

I'm Deaf or hard of hearing — how do I call a phone-only authority?

Use Canada VRS (free sign-language video relay, available 24/7) or dial 711 for a text-based relay operator — both let you place a real phone call to any number listed on this page.
