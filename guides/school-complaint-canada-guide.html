1. Teacher or Principal

Raise the issue directly and in writing first — most boards require this before accepting a formal complaint, and it creates a paper trail.

2. School Board / Trustee

Every Canadian school board publishes a formal complaints policy — ask for it by name. Your elected trustee's office can also help push a stalled issue.

3. Provincial Ombudsman or Ministry (If Unresolved)

In Ontario, the Ombudsman has overseen all 72 public school boards since 2015 — you can complain once the school and board have had a chance to respond.

Ontario Ombudsman — 1-800-263-1830 →

In British Columbia, the Ombudsperson has the same kind of oversight over schools and school districts.

BC Ombudsperson — 1-800-567-3247 →

In Manitoba, the Ombudsman reviews school divisions for administrative fairness, and for how they've handled your personal information or an access request.

Manitoba Ombudsman — 1-800-665-0531 →

In Saskatchewan, the Ombudsman does not have jurisdiction over schools, teachers, or school boards directly — only over the Ministry of Education itself.

Ombudsman Saskatchewan — Schools →

In Alberta, the Ombudsman does not cover schools at all. A complaint about a specific teacher's professional conduct goes to the Alberta Teaching Profession Commission instead — there's no external oversight body for general school or board decisions.

Alberta Teaching Profession Commission — 780-427-3663 →

In Quebec, an unresolved complaint goes to the Protecteur national de l'élève through your local centre de services scolaire's own complaints officer, in three stages. There's no single provincial phone line — start on your school board's own website.

Atlantic Canada

Nova Scotia: after the teacher and principal, go to your Regional Centre for Education (or CSAP). The Ombudsman reviews Regional Centres.

Nova Scotia — Office of the Ombudsman — 1-800-670-1111 →

New Brunswick: after the school, go to the district office. Ombud NB reviews school districts and District Education Councils.

New Brunswick — Ombud NB — 1-888-465-1100 →

PEI: teacher, principal, then the Public Schools Branch. The PEI Ombudsperson does not investigate school decisions.

PEI — Public Schools Branch →

Newfoundland & Labrador: teacher, principal, then NLSchools. The Citizens' Representative reviews the Department of Education.

Newfoundland & Labrador — NLSchools (Department of Education) — 709-729-1234 → Newfoundland & Labrador — Citizens' Representative →
Territories

Yukon: principal, superintendent or school council, then the Department. The Yukon Ombudsman covers public schools.

Yukon — Department of Education (public schools) — 867-667-5068 → Yukon — Education Appeal Tribunal → Yukon — Yukon Ombudsman — 867-667-8468 →

NWT: principal, then a written appeal to your local education body. The NWT Ombud covers education bodies.

NWT — NWT Ombud — 1-844-686-6283 →

Nunavut: principal, then your District Education Authority. Nunavut has no Ombudsman; the Representative for Children and Youth can review a student's issue with a government service.

Nunavut — Representative for Children and Youth — 1-855-449-8118 →

Special Education (IEP) Delays

A stalled assessment or unmet accommodation — raise it in writing with the school's special education resource teacher first, then the board's special education department. In Ontario, unresolved IEP disputes can go to the Special Education Tribunal or the Ontario Ombudsman.

Frequently Asked

How do I file a complaint against a school in Canada?

Start with the teacher or principal directly. If unresolved, escalate in writing to the school board. In Ontario, the Ombudsman has overseen school board complaints since 2015.

How do I complain about my child's school board?

Contact the board's office or your elected trustee. Every board publishes a formal complaints policy on its website — ask for it by name.

What if my child isn't getting the special education support in their IEP?

Raise it in writing with the special education resource teacher first, then the board's special education department. In Ontario, unresolved disputes can go to the Special Education Tribunal or the Ombudsman.

Where do I complain about bullying at school?

Report it to the teacher and principal in writing and ask what the school's bullying prevention policy requires. Escalate to the board if the school doesn't act.

Is there an Ombudsman for schools in Ontario?

Yes — since 2015, Ontario's Ombudsman has overseen all 72 public school boards, once the school and board have had a chance to respond first.

How do I report a teacher to the school board?

Put your concern in writing to the principal first — most boards require this before they accept a formal complaint. If it isn't resolved, file under the board's formal complaints policy and copy your elected trustee. In Alberta, a complaint about a teacher's professional conduct goes to the Alberta Teaching Profession Commission (780-427-3663).

How do I complain about a school principal?

Write to the principal first so there's a record, then escalate to the school board — ask for its formal complaints policy by name. Your trustee's office can help push a stalled complaint, and in Ontario, BC and Manitoba the provincial Ombudsman can review it if the board doesn't resolve it.

Should I complain to the Ministry of Education or the school board?

Usually the school board (also called a board of education or school division) — it's the level that handles complaints about schools and staff. If the board doesn't resolve it, go to your provincial Ombudsman where it has jurisdiction. In Saskatchewan, the Ombudsman covers only the Ministry of Education itself, not schools or boards.

I'm Deaf or hard of hearing — how do I call a phone-only authority?

Use Canada VRS (free sign-language video relay, available 24/7) or dial 711 for a text-based relay operator — both let you place a real phone call to any number listed on this page.
