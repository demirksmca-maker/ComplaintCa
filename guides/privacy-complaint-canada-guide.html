1. The Organization Directly

Ask for their privacy officer — most organizations are required to have one, and many complaints resolve at this stage.

2. Office of the Privacy Commissioner of Canada (OPC)

For private companies (outside BC/Alberta/Quebec) and federal institutions.

File with the OPC — 1-800-282-1376 →

3. Your Provincial Commissioner

Ontario's IPC, BC's OIPC, Alberta's OIPC, or Quebec's Commission d'accès à l'information — ask Asya with your province for the exact contact.

Private company (most provinces): OPC, federal, under PIPEDA.
Private company in BC, AB, or QC: that province's own commissioner.
Federal institution: OPC, under the Privacy Act.
Provincial/municipal body: your province's Information and Privacy Commissioner.

Common Privacy Complaint Types

  • Data breach — your information was exposed and the organization didn't notify you, or you believe they mishandled the response.
  • Unauthorized use or sharing — your data used or sold beyond what you consented to.
  • Access request denied — an organization refused a request to see what personal data it holds about you.
  • Government file mishandling — a public body exposed or mismanaged your personal file.

Frequently Asked

How do I file a privacy complaint in Canada?

Raise it with the organization's privacy officer first. If unresolved, file with the OPC (private companies, federal bodies) or your provincial commissioner (BC, Alberta, Quebec, or any public body).

What is PIPEDA and who does it apply to?

Canada's federal private-sector privacy law — covers most businesses, except in BC, Alberta, and Quebec, which have their own similar laws.

How do I report a data breach?

Ask the organization if they've notified the Privacy Commissioner, as required for breaches posing real risk of harm. You can also file your own complaint with the OPC or your provincial commissioner.

Where do I complain about a government agency mishandling my data?

Federal institutions fall under the OPC and the Privacy Act. Provincial or municipal bodies (like a hospital or school board) fall under your province's own privacy commissioner.

What is ComplaintCA?
