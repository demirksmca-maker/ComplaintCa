Three Ways to Get Free or Low-Cost Legal Help

1. Law Society Referral Service — matched with a licensed lawyer or paralegal for a short first consultation, usually free or a flat $25–$30 fee. Not government-funded — you pay for further work.

2. Legal Aid — government-funded. If you meet income/asset limits and your issue is covered (mainly criminal, family, immigration/refugee), it can fund part or all of an actual lawyer.

3. Pro Bono Programs — independent non-profits organizing volunteer lawyers for civil matters like housing or employment, usually free, limited availability.

Is There a Federal Office?

No single federal legal aid office — it's delivered by each province/territory. Justice Canada cost-shares funding, including immigration and refugee (I&R) legal aid agreements with ON, BC, AB, MB, QC, NL, and NS.

Frequently Asked

What is the Law Society Referral Service?

A free matching service from your province's Law Society that connects you with a lawyer or paralegal for a short first consultation — usually free or a small flat fee, but not funded legal representation.

What is the difference between a lawyer referral service and legal aid?

A referral service only sets up a brief consultation; you pay for further work yourself. Legal aid can fund actual representation, but only if you meet income limits and have a covered legal issue.

How much does legal aid cost in Canada?

It varies by province and income — many low-income applicants pay nothing, while others pay a contribution (e.g. roughly $25–$100 in BC, or $100–$800 in Quebec) based on income and family size.

Am I eligible for legal aid in Canada?

It depends on your household income/assets and whether your issue is a covered type — mainly criminal, family, and immigration/refugee matters, with some provinces also covering tenant disputes.

What is pro bono legal help?

Volunteer lawyers helping for free through an organized program, usually for civil issues like housing or employment, independent of government legal aid.

Is legal aid the same as a free lawyer?

Not exactly — legal aid can require a contribution, a referral service is only a free short consultation, and pro bono programs are the option most likely to be fully free but have limited availability.

Is there a federal legal aid office in Canada?

No single federal office — legal aid is delivered provincially/territorially, though Justice Canada cost-shares funding, including immigration and refugee legal aid agreements with ON, BC, AB, MB, QC, NL, and NS.
