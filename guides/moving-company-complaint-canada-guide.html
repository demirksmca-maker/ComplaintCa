Mover Is Holding Your Belongings

Some rogue movers load your things, then demand much more than the written estimate before they unload.

Stay calm and don't sign anything new. Call your local police non-emergency line and show them your written estimate. Then report the company to the Canadian Anti-Fraud Centre and your provincial consumer office.

Canadian Anti-Fraud Centre — 1-888-495-8501 →

Damaged or Lost Items

Note any damage on the delivery papers before the movers leave. Take photos right away.

Send a written claim to the moving company. Check your contract (bill of lading) for the claim deadline. Movers' basic coverage is often paid by weight, not by value, unless you bought extra protection.

Quick Answer

Complain to the moving company in writing first and keep your estimate, contract and photos. For overcharges or bad service, go to your provincial consumer office, such as Consumer Protection Ontario (1-800-889-9768) or Consumer Protection BC (1-888-564-9963). For damage the company won't pay for, use Small Claims Court. If a mover holds your things for more money, call local police and report it to the Canadian Anti-Fraud Centre (1-888-495-8501).

Before You Hire a Mover

  • Get at least three written estimates, ideally after an in-home or video visit.
  • Be careful with very low phone quotes and large cash deposits.
  • Check reviews and the company's real address.
  • Ask what their coverage pays for damage, and whether you need extra protection.
  • Read the contract before moving day, and keep a copy.

Frequently Asked

What can I do if a mover holds my belongings for more money?

Don't sign anything new. Call your local police non-emergency line and show them your written estimate. Report the company to the Canadian Anti-Fraud Centre (1-888-495-8501) and your provincial consumer office.

How do I claim for damage caused by movers?

Note the damage on the delivery papers, take photos, and send a written claim to the company before the deadline in your contract. If they refuse, you can go to Small Claims Court.

Where do I file a complaint against a moving company?

With your provincial consumer protection office, for example Consumer Protection Ontario (1-800-889-9768), Consumer Protection BC (1-888-564-9963), Service Alberta (1-877-427-4088) or the OPC in Quebec.

Does the Canadian Association of Movers help with complaints?

It can help when the mover is a CAM member. Check membership on its website.

Why did the movers only offer a small amount for my damaged item?

Basic coverage is often paid by weight, not value. You only get more if you bought extra protection or can prove the company was at fault in court.
