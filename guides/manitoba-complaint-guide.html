Quick Answer

Consumer problems go to the Manitoba Consumer Protection Office (1-800-782-0067). Rental disputes go to the Residential Tenancies Branch (1-800-782-8403). Complaints about a provincial department go to the Manitoba Ombudsman (1-800-665-0531). Money claims up to $20,000 can go to Small Claims Court. Always complain to the business or office first and keep a written record.

Manitoba Rules You Should Know

Damage deposits. When a tenancy ends, the landlord must return the security deposit, with interest, within 14 days. A landlord who wants to keep part of it must send a written claim to both you and the Residential Tenancies Branch within 28 days of move-out. If no claim arrives in that window, ask the Branch about getting your full deposit back.

Door-to-door and direct sales. If a seller signed you up at your home, you can cancel within 10 days in writing and get a full refund. If the seller was not licensed in Manitoba, that window extends to one year. The Consumer Protection Office can tell you whether a seller holds a licence.

Online orders that never arrive. Under Manitoba's Consumer Protection Act, you can cancel an internet purchase if the goods or services are not delivered within 30 days after the promised delivery date. Send the cancellation in writing and keep a copy.

What the Ombudsman can and cannot do. The Manitoba Ombudsman looks at provincial departments, Crown corporations, health authorities and municipalities. It does not handle private businesses, federal services, or court decisions. Complaints must be in writing, and the office may decline a matter you knew about more than a year earlier or one you could still appeal elsewhere.

Discrimination. A complaint to the Manitoba Human Rights Commission must be filed within one year of the incident, or of the most recent incident if the treatment is ongoing.

Example: A Winnipeg Tenant's Deposit

Sam moves out of a Winnipeg apartment on June 30. By mid-July the $600 deposit hasn't come back and the landlord hasn't replied to texts. Sam's first step is a short written request to the landlord, keeping a copy. If no written claim from the landlord reaches Sam and the Residential Tenancies Branch by July 28, Sam contacts the Branch at 1-800-782-8403 with the lease, move-out photos and the letter. The Branch, not a court, is the right place for this dispute.

Common Mistakes in Manitoba

  • Going to Small Claims Court for a rental dispute that belongs at the Residential Tenancies Branch.
  • Cancelling a direct-sale contract by phone only, with no written notice inside the 10 days.
  • Asking the Ombudsman to act against a private company. Use the Consumer Protection Office instead.
  • Waiting too long on discrimination: the one-year clock starts at the incident, not when you decide to complain.

Frequently Asked

Where do I file a consumer complaint in Manitoba?

The Manitoba Consumer Protection Office, at 1-800-782-0067.

Where do I file a tenancy dispute in Manitoba?

The Residential Tenancies Branch, at 1-800-782-8403.

Where do I complain about a Manitoba government department?

The Manitoba Ombudsman, at 1-800-665-0531.

What is the small claims limit in Manitoba?

$20,000, since January 1, 2025. Claims above that go to the Court of King's Bench, or you can give up the extra amount and stay in small claims.

Where do I file a human rights complaint in Manitoba?

The Manitoba Human Rights Commission takes complaints about discrimination in jobs, housing and services. File within one year of the last incident.

Do I have to complain to the business first?

Yes. The Consumer Protection Office and most other bodies expect you to give the business a chance to fix it first. Put it in writing and keep a copy.

What is ComplaintCA?

Where do I report cyber crime in Canada?

The Canadian Anti-Fraud Centre (1-888-495-8501) and your local police service. ComplaintCA's Cyber Crime module helps you document the report first.

I'm Deaf or hard of hearing — how do I call a phone-only authority?

Use Canada VRS (free sign-language video relay, available 24/7) or dial 711 for a text-based relay operator — both let you place a real phone call to any number listed on this page.
