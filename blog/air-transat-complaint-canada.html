Air Transat Complaint? Claim First, Then the CTA

Published November 3, 2026 · Updated November 3, 2026 · 3 min read

Short answer: claim in writing with Air Transat first. If it refuses or does not answer within 30 days, file a free complaint with the Canadian Transportation Agency (CTA). You have 1 year for a delayed or cancelled flight.

In This Guide
→1: Claim First→2: CTA→Limits→The Wait→FAQ→Sources

Step 1: Claim with the airline

Send your claim to Air Transat in writing. Include your booking details (ticket, invoice, flight number) and proof of the delay and the reason given.

Keep a copy and note the date you sent it.

↑ Back to sections

Step 2: File with the CTA

If the airline denies your claim or you hear nothing after 30 days, file through the CTA's air travel complaints process. It is free.

Include proof that you contacted the airline first.

↑ Back to sections

What the CTA cannot do

The CTA can address flight incidents such as delays. It cannot award compensation for pain and suffering or loss of income.

Check the CTA site for current compensation amounts before you quote a figure.

↑ Back to sections

Expect a queue

The CTA has a long backlog, so file as soon as your window opens. See our report on the CTA backlog and the Air Canada example.

Frequently Asked

How do I complain about Air Transat?

Claim in writing with Air Transat first. If it refuses or does not answer in 30 days, file a free complaint with the CTA.

How long do I have to claim?

One year for a delayed or cancelled flight, according to the sources we checked. Confirm on the CTA site.

Can the CTA award compensation for stress?

No. It cannot award compensation for pain and suffering or loss of income.

Do I need a lawyer?

No. You can file with the CTA yourself.
