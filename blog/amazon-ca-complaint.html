How to File an Amazon.ca Complaint (and an A-to-z Claim)

Published October 25, 2026 · 4 min read
A-to-z
Amazon's guarantee for orders from third-party sellers

On Amazon.ca, many items are sold by other sellers. If the seller does not fix your problem, Amazon's A-to-z Guarantee lets you ask Amazon to step in. In Canada it also covers property damage or injury from a defective product.

Step 1: Where to Complain

Step 2: Put It in Writing

Message the seller first and give them about 2 days to answer. Keep screenshots of the order page, the tracking and every message. Amazon looks at this when it decides your A-to-z claim.

Say what happened, when, what you already tried and exactly what you want back. Our complaint letter template shows the six parts every complaint needs.

Step 3: If They Say No

  • Refund refused or misleading sale: file with your provincial consumer office. In Ontario that is Consumer Protection Ontario, 1-800-889-9768, weekdays 8:30 a.m. to 5 p.m.
  • Paid by credit card and the item never came or was not as described: ask your card issuer about a chargeback. See our chargeback guide.
  • Larger amounts: Small Claims Court. Ontario's limit is $50,000. See the Small Claims guide.

Frequently Asked

How do I contact Amazon.ca customer service?

Sign in, go to Help, then Contact us. You can choose chat or ask Amazon to call you back.

When can I file an A-to-z Guarantee claim?

When you bought from a third-party seller and the seller did not resolve the problem after you contacted them, for example an item that never arrived or is not as described.

A defective product damaged my property. Does Amazon cover it?

In Canada, the A-to-z Guarantee also covers property damage and personal injury caused by a defective product bought on Amazon.ca. Contact Amazon customer service to file.

Amazon closed my claim. What now?

If you paid by credit card, ask your card issuer about a chargeback. You can also file with your provincial consumer office or go to Small Claims Court.
