The headline number is genuinely better. But "backlog" is an average across categories moving in opposite directions — and if you're in the wrong one, the wait didn't get shorter at all.

October 2026 Update

IRCC's April 30, 2026 data showed a total inventory of 2,153,900 applications, with 922,700 in backlog (past service standards). That is the lowest backlog level since July 2025. Permanent residence and citizenship remain the slowest areas, so check your own category on IRCC's processing-times page before you escalate.

What's Actually Happening

IRCC's total application inventory across all programs stood at 2,154,300 as of March 31, 2026, with a 43% overall backlog rate. Express Entry — the fastest economic-class stream — improved to a backlog of just 10%, with a service standard of 6 months for 80% of applications.

Family sponsorship has been trending in the opposite direction through 2026, as IRCC redirects processing resources toward clearing the economic-class backlog. Humanitarian and Compassionate applications and the Federal Self-Employed and Start-Up Visa programs are the worst off: processing times exceeding ten years in most cases.

The Real Problem: There's Still No Ombudsman

Unlike telecom (CCTS) or banking (OBSI), immigration has no independent ombudsman you can escalate a stalled file to. IRCC's own service standards are targets, not enforceable deadlines — so a file sitting well past the stated processing time has no independent body to appeal to.

What Actually Works If You're Stuck

Use the official webform to request an update once you're past IRCC's stated processing time for your category — it's the documented first step.

Contact your MP's office. Immigration is one of the most common file types MPs' offices handle, and they can request a status update on your behalf through official channels.

For extreme delays, a mandamus application (asking the Federal Court to compel a decision) becomes realistic once a file has sat well beyond service standards with no explanation — worth a real legal consultation before filing.

Preparing while your file waits

A long queue is a good time to make sure nothing expires. Keep a calendar of passport validity, medical exam and police certificate dates, and check whether any document will need renewing before a decision is likely. Update IRCC promptly if your address, family situation or job changes.

Use IRCC's processing-times tool to see where your category stands before you write, and quote the application number in every message. Keep a short file of what you sent and when, so a webform or MP request can be answered from facts instead of memory.

Frequently Asked

How big is the IRCC backlog in 2026?

IRCC's April 30, 2026 data showed a total inventory of 2,153,900 applications, with 922,700 in backlog (past service standards). That is the lowest backlog level since July 2025.

Is there an ombudsman for IRCC delays?

No. Unlike telecom (CCTS) or banking (OBSI), immigration has no independent ombudsman. IRCC's service standards are targets, not enforceable deadlines.

What can I do if my IRCC file is stuck?

Once you are past the stated processing time for your category, use IRCC's official webform to request an update. You can also ask your MP's office to request a status update. For extreme delays, a mandamus application to the Federal Court may become realistic, so get legal advice first.

Which immigration categories are slowest?

Permanent residence and citizenship remain the slowest areas. Humanitarian and Compassionate applications and the Federal Self-Employed and Start-Up Visa programs have processing times above ten years in most cases. Express Entry is the fastest, with a 6-month service standard for 80% of applications.
