Fake Black Friday Discount? How to Report It in Canada

Published November 9, 2026 · Updated November 9, 2026 · 4 min read

Short answer: report it to the Competition Bureau with its online complaint form or at 1-800-348-5358. Under the Competition Act, the "regular" price in a sale ad has to be a price the product really sold at, or was really offered at, for a meaningful stretch of time. An invented one is illegal.

the line a "regular price" must clear: more than half of sales over 12 months, or more than half of the time over 6 months, at or above that price.

A strikethrough price is a promise that you are saving money. If the "was" figure was inflated the week before the sale, or never charged at all, the saving is fiction. Canadian law treats that as deceptive marketing, and you can report it in about ten minutes.

Step 1 — Spot a Fake "Regular" Price

The Competition Bureau's guideline gives a store two ways to justify the price it compares against. It only has to pass one:

Volume test: more than 50% of the product's sales were at or above the reference price, counted over the 12 months before or after the ad.

Time test: the product was offered in good faith at that price for more than half of the 6 months before or after the ad.

Seasonal goods can use a shorter period. Since a 2024 change to the Competition Act, it is the seller who has to show its claim passes one of the tests.

Warning signs: a "was" price you have never seen in the store, a price that jumped a few days before the sale, a "compare at" figure with no store named, and countdown timers that reset when you reload the page.

Step 2 — Save Proof Before the Sale Ends

Sale pages change or vanish once the event is over, so collect evidence while it is live:

  1. Screenshot the product page with the address bar, date and both prices visible.
  2. Keep the flyer, email or social media ad that showed the deal.
  3. Find older proof of the real price: an earlier screenshot, an old flyer, or a price you saved in a wish list.
  4. Keep your order confirmation and receipt if you bought it.

Step 3 — Report It to the Competition Bureau

File through the Bureau's online complaint form, or call 1-800-348-5358. Name the store, the product, the date, the two prices, and attach your screenshots.

Reports matter. The Bureau has taken retailers to task over price claims before: Amazon agreed to pay a $1 million penalty plus $100,000 in costs over savings claims based on list prices, and The Dufresne Group paid $3.25 million in 2023 to settle concerns about its marketing claims.

The Bureau investigates businesses. It does not chase a refund for one shopper, which is why Step 4 exists.

Step 4 — Get Your Own Money Back

Write to the store first. Ask for a refund or a price adjustment and say the "regular" price was misleading. Give it a deadline, such as 10 business days.

If it says no, go to your provincial consumer office: Consumer Protection Ontario (1-800-889-9768), Consumer Protection BC (1-888-564-9963) or Quebec's Office de la protection du consommateur (1-888-672-2556). If you paid by card, a dispute with your card issuer is another route; see what to do if a chargeback is denied.

Turn your screenshots into a clear, dated complaint for the store or the Competition Bureau.

Frequently Asked

Is a fake Black Friday discount illegal in Canada?

Yes. The Competition Act bans materially false or misleading claims about a product's ordinary or regular price. A store must be able to show the regular price passes the volume test or the time test.

What are the volume test and the time test?

Volume test: more than 50% of sales at or above the regular price over the 12 months before or after the ad. Time test: offered in good faith at that price for more than half of the 6 months before or after. Passing one is enough.

How do I report a fake sale price?

Use the Competition Bureau's online complaint form or call 1-800-348-5358. Include the store, product, date, both prices and your screenshots.

Will the Competition Bureau get my money back?

No. The Bureau investigates businesses but does not recover money for individual shoppers. Ask the store for a refund in writing, then contact your provincial consumer office or your card issuer.

What penalties have stores paid for fake sale prices?

Amazon agreed to a $1 million penalty plus $100,000 in costs over list-price savings claims, and The Dufresne Group paid $3.25 million in 2023 to settle the Bureau's concerns over its marketing claims.
