Gift Card Expired in Ontario? Most Cards Cannot Expire

Published November 16, 2026 · Updated November 16, 2026 · 4 min read
By Kasım Demir · Founder, ComplaintCA

Short answer: in Ontario, most store and restaurant gift cards cannot expire, and the business cannot charge fees that eat the balance. If a store refuses your card, ask in writing, then complain to Consumer Protection Ontario at 1-800-889-9768.

15 months
How long a shopping mall gift card keeps its full value in Ontario before a monthly fee can start. Ordinary store cards never lose value to fees.

Can a gift card expire in Ontario?

Usually not. Ontario's Consumer Protection Act bans expiry dates on most retail gift cards sold for a dollar value. The ban has applied since October 1, 2007.

If an expiry date is printed on the card anyway, the regulation (O. Reg. 17/05) treats the card as if it had no expiry date. The balance is still yours.

The same rules stop a business from shrinking the balance with fees:

  • Not allowed: activation fees, service fees and fees that reduce the value over time (dormancy fees).
  • Allowed: a fee to customize a card, and a fee to replace a lost or stolen card.

Which gift cards can still expire?

Type of cardWhat the rules say
Store or restaurant card with a dollar valueNo expiry date. No activation or dormancy fees.
Shopping mall card (works at many stores)Fees can start after 15 months. See the next section.
Card for one specific good or service, such as one massage or one manicureCan have an expiry date.
Card issued for a charitable purposeCan have an expiry date.
Loyalty, points or rewards cardNot covered by the gift card rules.
Prepaid credit card from a bank or card networkFederal rules apply, not Ontario's gift card rules.

Not sure which one you have? Read the back of the card. A dollar amount you can spend on anything in the store points to the first row.

Shopping mall gift cards: the 15-month rule

Mall cards are the one common gift card that can lose value in Ontario. The limits are:

  1. An activation fee of up to $1.50 when the card is bought.
  2. Full value for 15 months from the date of purchase.
  3. After that, a dormancy fee of up to $2.50 a month.
  4. You can ask for a 3-month extension before fees start. Contact the card supplier during the 15th month after purchase, before the 15 months run out.

The fees and conditions must be printed on the card. At the maximum fee, an untouched $50 mall card is empty 20 months after the fees begin.

Simplest fix: spend mall cards first, and note the purchase date on the card sleeve.

What to do if a store refuses your card

  1. Ask for the manager and quote the rule. “This card was sold with a dollar value. Under Ontario's Consumer Protection Act it cannot have an expiry date. Please honour the balance of $[amount].”
  2. Write to the business. Email or letter. Attach a photo of both sides of the card and the receipt if you have it. Say what you want and by what date. Keep a copy.
  3. File with Consumer Protection Ontario. The ministry asks you to contact the business first. Then file online at ontario.ca or call 416-326-8800 or toll-free 1-800-889-9768. Include your letter, any reply and your proof.

A card you received as a gift is treated the same way as one you bought yourself, so use the same steps.

Outside Ontario, or a prepaid Visa or Mastercard?

Other provinces have their own gift card rules. If your card was bought elsewhere, check with that province's consumer protection office before you write.

Prepaid credit cards are regulated by the federal government. The Financial Consumer Agency of Canada explains those rules on its gift cards page. If the problem is a refused return and not the card itself, see store refund rules in Canada.

Frequently Asked

Can a gift card expire in Ontario?

Most cannot. Ontario's Consumer Protection Act bans expiry dates on most retail gift cards sold for a dollar value. A printed expiry date is treated as if it were not there.

Which gift cards are allowed to expire in Ontario?

Cards for one specific good or service, such as one massage, and cards issued for a charitable purpose. Loyalty cards are not covered by the gift card rules, and prepaid credit cards follow federal rules.

Can a store charge fees on a gift card in Ontario?

No activation, service or dormancy fees are allowed on ordinary store gift cards. A business may charge to customize a card or to replace a lost or stolen one.

When does a mall gift card start losing value in Ontario?

After 15 months from the purchase date. A shopping mall card can then lose up to $2.50 a month. Contact the card supplier during the 15th month to ask for a 3-month extension.

Who do I complain to about a gift card in Ontario?

Write to the business first. If it does not fix the problem, file a complaint with Consumer Protection Ontario at 416-326-8800 or 1-800-889-9768.
