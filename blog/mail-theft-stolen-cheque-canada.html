Mail Stolen or a Cheque Cashed by Someone Else? What to Do in Canada

Published October 12, 2026 · Updated November 3, 2026 · 5 min read

Short answer: report it to your local police and to Canada Post customer service at 1-866-607-6301. Theft from the mail is a crime under section 356 of the Criminal Code. Money first: call your bank, then build the paper trail.

is the maximum prison sentence for theft from the mail under section 356 of Canada's Criminal Code. Fraudulently redirecting someone's mail is covered by the same section.

A cheque you mailed never arrived, then turned up cashed by a stranger. Or your mailbox was broken into and your bank statements are gone. Mail theft feeds cheque fraud and identity theft, so the order of your calls matters. Money first, then the paper trail.

Step by Step

1Call your bank today. If a cheque was taken, ask the bank to stop payment on any cheque that hasn't cleared, and to open a fraud claim for one that was altered or cashed. Banks set their own deadlines for reporting a forged or altered cheque, so don't wait for your next statement. Get a reference number.

2Report it to Canada Post. Call customer service at 1-866-607-6301 or open a ticket on the Canada Post support page. Say whether it was a community mailbox, a house box or an item in transit. If you got a change-of-address notice you didn't ask for, say so.

3File a police report. Theft from the mail is a federal crime. A police report number helps with your bank claim and shows a pattern if your neighbours were hit too. If you aren't sure the letter or notice you received is real, run it through our Verify a Document check first.

4Report to the Canadian Anti-Fraud Centre. Call 1-888-495-8501 or use the online reporting system. If your ID or tax slips were in the stolen mail, you can also log it in our Cyber Crime Report section.

5Protect your credit file. Ask Equifax and TransUnion to add a fraud alert, and check your credit report for accounts you didn't open.

Frequently Asked

Who investigates mail theft in Canada?

Police investigate mail theft, and the Canada Post Corporation Security and Investigation Services also looks into it. Report it to your local police and to Canada Post customer service at 1-866-607-6301. Theft from the mail is a crime under section 356 of the Criminal Code.

Who do I report stolen mail to in Canada?

Report it to Canada Post customer service at 1-866-607-6301 and to your local police. Theft from the mail is a crime under section 356 of the Criminal Code, with a maximum of 10 years in prison.

What if someone cashed a cheque I mailed?

Call your bank the same day you find out. Banks set their own deadlines for reporting altered or forged cheques, and a late report can cost you the refund. Then report it to the police and the Canadian Anti-Fraud Centre at 1-888-495-8501.

Can someone redirect my mail without my permission?

Fraudulently redirecting mail is an offence under section 356 of the Criminal Code. If you get a change-of-address notice you didn't request, or your mail suddenly stops, call Canada Post and the police right away.

Should I put a fraud alert on my credit file?

If stolen mail contained bank statements, tax slips or ID, yes. Call Equifax Canada at 1-800-465-7166 and TransUnion Canada at 1-800-663-9980. You have to ask each bureau separately.

What if someone deposited my stolen cheque with a mobile app?

Call your bank's fraud line right away and say the cheque was stolen and deposited by someone else. Ask the bank to investigate, reverse the payment if it can, and confirm in writing. Then report it to police and the Canadian Anti-Fraud Centre at 1-888-495-8501, and keep the report numbers.

Reducing the chance it happens again

Once a cheque or a bank letter has been stolen, assume the thief may try again. Ask the sender to use direct deposit or electronic payment instead of cheques, and pick up important mail promptly rather than leaving it in a box overnight. If you travel, a hold-mail service lets Canada Post keep your items safely.

Tell Canada Post about suspected theft from a community mailbox so it can investigate, and keep the police file number when you report it. For a cheque you wrote, ask your bank to stop payment and to watch the account for related activity.

Proving what you wrote

If a cheque was altered, ask the bank for images of the front and back of the cashed item and compare the payee and amount with your records. Keep the cheque register or a photo of the stub. Those details are what a bank uses to decide whether it should reverse the payment.
