Got a Call From the “RCMP”? It’s Probably a Scam. Here’s What to Do

Published November 6, 2026 · Updated November 6, 2026 · 4 min read

Short answer: hang up. Real police never ask for money, gift cards, crypto or your bank card over the phone, and the number on your screen can be faked. Look up your local police or RCMP detachment yourself and call back. If you already paid, call your bank first.

The 3-step check that beats every fake police call

On November 6, 2026, Bashaw RCMP in Alberta warned that scammers were posing as RCMP officers, government staff, banks and “cybercrime investigators”, with calls made to look like they came from local detachments. Two weeks earlier, RCMP headquarters said it was getting reports from across the country of the same trick. Here is how it works and how to shut it down in under a minute.

How the Fake Police Call Works

  1. Your phone shows a real number, such as your local RCMP detachment. Caller ID can be spoofed, so this proves nothing.
  2. The caller sounds official. RCMP reports describe callers who claim to be sergeants and give fake regimental numbers.
  3. You are told you are the victim of a financial crime, or that your bank account is part of an investigation.
  4. Then comes the ask: your banking details, a transfer to a “safe” account, a payment, or a courier who will pick up your bank card.
  5. And the secret: you are told not to tell anyone, not even your bank.

Some versions use fake emails or letters with an official logo, including the Canadian Anti-Fraud Centre’s own branding.

What Real Police Never Do

According to the RCMP and the Canadian Anti-Fraud Centre, real police will never:

  • ⚠️ Ask for money or payment of any kind over the phone
  • ⚠️ Ask for gift cards, cryptocurrency or money transfers
  • ⚠️ Ask for your debit or credit card details, or send someone to collect your card
  • ⚠️ Ask you to move money to a “safe” account or send money to help an investigation
  • ⚠️ Ask you to keep an investigation secret
One of these is enough. If a caller asks for any item on this list, you are talking to a scammer, whatever the screen says.

Hang Up, Look Up, Call Back

  1. Hang up. You don’t need a reason. A line that works: “I’ll call the detachment back on its public number.”
  2. Look up the number yourself, on the police service’s official website. Never use a number the caller gives you.
  3. Call back and ask if the call was real. For anything about your bank, use the number on the back of your card.

Got a suspicious letter, email or website that uses a police or government logo? Check it with our Verify a Document or Scam Guide before you act.

If You Already Paid or Shared Details

  1. Call your bank now, on the number on your card. Ask them to freeze cards and stop any transfer. Our list of bank fraud lines has the numbers for RBC, TD, CIBC, BMO and more.
  2. Report it to your local police and to the Canadian Anti-Fraud Centre at 1-888-495-8501 or online.
  3. Protect your identity: ask Equifax and TransUnion for a fraud alert. Our identity theft steps walk you through it.
  4. Keep everything: call times, numbers, names, receipts and screenshots.
  5. Warn your family, especially older relatives. Scammers often call the same area again.

Frequently Asked

Can a scam call really show my local RCMP detachment’s number?

Yes. Caller ID can be spoofed. On November 6, 2026, Bashaw RCMP in Alberta warned that scam calls were made to look like they came from local RCMP detachments. Never trust the number on your screen; hang up and call back on a number you look up yourself.

Will the RCMP ever ask me for money over the phone?

No. The RCMP says officers will never ask for money over the phone, and police do not demand payment in gift cards, cryptocurrency or money transfers. Any caller who asks for payment is a scammer.

The caller says I must keep the investigation secret. Is that normal?

No. The Canadian Anti-Fraud Centre says it and the police will never ask you to keep an investigation confidential, move money to a “safe” account, or send money to help an investigation. Secrecy is a red flag.

Someone is coming to pick up my bank card. What should I do?

Do not hand it over. Real police and banks do not send couriers to collect cards. Call your bank on the number on the back of your card, then call your local police.

Where do I report a fake police call in Canada?

Report it to the Canadian Anti-Fraud Centre at 1-888-495-8501 or online, and to your local police. If you shared banking details or sent money, call your bank first, right away.
