How to Complain About a Doctor in Ontario: The CPSO Process, Step by Step

Published October 8, 2026 · 5 min read
No deadline
to complain about a doctor to the CPSO. But adult patient records only have to be kept for 10 years, so the sooner you file, the stronger your case.

The College of Physicians and Surgeons of Ontario (CPSO) licenses and disciplines every doctor in the province. A complaint is free, and it can lead to anything from advice to a discipline hearing. It can't get you money, so it helps to know what it can and can't do before you start.

Step by Step

1Try to resolve it first, where it makes sense. Speak with the doctor or the clinic manager. In a hospital, contact its patient experience representative. In a Family Health Team, contact its administration.

2Gather the facts. Write down dates, what happened, who was there, and what you want. You have a right to a copy of your medical records.

3File with the CPSO. Use the complaint form on the CPSO website, or call 1-800-268-7096 ext. 603 (416-967-2603), Monday to Friday, 8 a.m. to 5 p.m.

4The investigation. The CPSO notifies the doctor, an investigator gathers information, the doctor responds, and the Inquiries, Complaints and Reports Committee issues a written decision with reasons to you and the doctor.

5Disagree? Ask for a review within 30 days. You can ask the Health Professions Appeal and Review Board (HPARB), in writing, to review the decision within 30 days of receiving the notice of your right to a review.

Possible Outcomes

  • No further action
  • Advice, or remedial self-study
  • An undertaking by the doctor
  • Required courses
  • A verbal caution
  • Referral to the Ontario Physicians and Surgeons Discipline Tribunal
What the CPSO can't do: award money, step into patient care decisions such as medication or referrals, or resolve insurance disputes. It also doesn't oversee hospitals, long-term care homes, nurses, personal support workers or pharmacists. For those, the CPSO refers people to the Patient Ombudsman. For sexual abuse by a doctor, call 1-800-268-7096 ext. 629 to speak with a social worker.

Write a clear, dated account of what happened before you file. It is the core of your complaint.

Frequently Asked

Is there a deadline to complain about a doctor in Ontario?

No. The CPSO says there is no time limit. But doctors only have to keep adult patients' records for 10 years, and documents, witnesses and memories get harder to find over time, so contact the CPSO as soon as you can.

Can the CPSO get me compensation?

No. The CPSO says it cannot award financial damages or step into patient care decisions such as medication or referrals. To seek money, you would need a separate civil lawsuit.

What can happen to a doctor after a CPSO complaint?

The Inquiries, Complaints and Reports Committee can take no further action, give advice or require remedial self-study, accept an undertaking, require courses, issue a verbal caution, or refer the doctor to the Ontario Physicians and Surgeons Discipline Tribunal.

Can I challenge the CPSO's decision?

In most cases, yes. You or the doctor can ask the Health Professions Appeal and Review Board (HPARB) to review the committee's decision, in writing, within 30 days of receiving the notice of your right to a review. Referrals to discipline or incapacity proceedings can't be reviewed.
