Complaint Against a Condo Board in Ontario? How the Condo Authority Tribunal Works

Published November 23, 2026 · Updated November 23, 2026 · 4 min read
By Kasım Demir · Founder, ComplaintCA

Short answer: raise it with your condo board or property manager in writing first. If it is not fixed and the issue is one the Tribunal covers, file online with the Condominium Authority Tribunal (CAT). Fees: $25 to file, $50 for mediation and $125 for a decision, so $200 at most.

Quick steps

  1. Write to the board or property manager. Name the rule in the declaration, by-laws or rules that you say is not being followed.
  2. Check that the Condominium Authority Tribunal (CAT) covers your issue on the Condominium Authority of Ontario website.
  3. Create a CAO account and file online. The $25 fee starts Stage 1, negotiation.
  4. No deal? Pay $50 to move to Stage 2, mediation.
  5. Still no deal? Pay $125 for Stage 3, where a Tribunal member decides.

Step 1: Write to the board

Send a short, polite letter or email to the board or property manager. Keep a copy.

  • What the problem is, with dates.
  • Which provision of the declaration, by-laws or rules it breaks.
  • What you want done, and by when.

Keep photos, emails and logs. The Tribunal will ask for them.

What the Tribunal can hear

The CAT hears certain condo disputes. Examples listed by the Condominium Authority of Ontario include:

  • Condo records requests.
  • Smoke and vapour rules.
  • Harassment, where the governing documents prohibit it.
  • Short-term rental rules.
  • Parking and storage (owners, mortgagees and the corporation only).
  • Settlement agreements reached at the Tribunal.

The dispute must be tied to a specific provision in the condo's governing documents. Check the full list on the CAO website.

The three stages and the fees

  • Stage 1, negotiation ($25): you and the other side try to settle online.
  • Stage 2, mediation ($50): a Tribunal mediator helps.
  • Stage 3, adjudication ($125): a Tribunal member hears both sides and decides.

A case that goes through all three stages costs $200 in Tribunal fees.

If the Tribunal cannot hear it

For issues outside the CAT's list, the Condominium Act requires mediation and arbitration for many disagreements between owners and the corporation about the declaration, by-laws or rules. Some matters go to court.

If you rent a condo unit, problems with your landlord go to the Landlord and Tenant Board instead.

Frequently Asked

How much does the Condominium Authority Tribunal cost?

$25 to file, $50 to move to mediation and $125 to move to a decision: $200 at most.

Do I have to contact the condo board first?

Yes, you should. Write to the board or manager and keep a copy. The Tribunal will want to see that you tried.

What issues can the CAT hear?

Examples include condo records, smoke and vapour, harassment, short-term rentals, parking and storage. The issue must be tied to a provision in the governing documents.

How do I file with the CAT?

Online, through a personal Condominium Authority of Ontario account.

I rent a condo. Where do I complain about my landlord?

The Landlord and Tenant Board, not the CAT.
