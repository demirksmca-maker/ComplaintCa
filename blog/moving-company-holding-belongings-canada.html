Moving Company Holding Your Belongings Hostage? What to Do in Canada

Published November 23, 2026 · Updated November 23, 2026 · 4 min read
By Kasım Demir · Founder, ComplaintCA

Short answer: if a mover will not release your things unless you pay much more than the quote, call the police and report it to the Canadian Anti-Fraud Centre at 1-888-495-8501. Keep the written quote. Then complain to the Canadian Association of Movers and the BBB, and claim your money back by chargeback or in small claims court.

Quick steps

  1. Do not sign new papers. Photograph the truck, plates and any new price demand.
  2. Call the police. Call 911 if anyone threatens you.
  3. Report it to the Canadian Anti-Fraud Centre: 1-888-495-8501.
  4. Complain to the Canadian Association of Movers and the Better Business Bureau.
  5. Paid by credit card? Ask your card issuer for a chargeback. Otherwise, sue in small claims court.

How the "rogue mover" scam works

The Competition Bureau warns about rogue movers who find customers online with a low price and "no hidden fees", then hold your belongings until you pay more for delivery.

Moving companies are not licensed or regulated as an industry in Canada, so your written quote and contract are your main proof.

What to do on moving day

  • Stay calm. Do not sign a new contract or a "release" form.
  • Ask for the new charges in writing, or record them.
  • Photograph the truck, licence plate, the crew and any invoice.
  • Call the police. They may call it a civil matter, but get a report or incident number.

Where to report

  • Police: if your goods are held for ransom.
  • Canadian Anti-Fraud Centre: 1-888-495-8501 or its online reporting system.
  • Competition Bureau: reports of misleading prices.
  • Canadian Association of Movers (CAM): industry complaints.
  • Better Business Bureau: a public complaint record.

Getting your money back

  • Credit card: ask your card issuer for a chargeback. Send the quote and the new demand.
  • Small claims court: sue for the extra charges or damage. In Ontario the limit is $50,000.

Next time: get at least three quotes, ask for an in-home estimate and a written contract, and avoid large cash deposits.

Frequently Asked

What do I do if movers hold my belongings for more money?

Do not sign anything new, call the police and report it to the Canadian Anti-Fraud Centre at 1-888-495-8501.

Are moving companies regulated in Canada?

No. The moving industry is not licensed or regulated as such, so get a written quote and contract.

Will the police help?

They may treat it as a civil matter. Still call and get an incident number for your complaint and court claim.

How can I get my money back?

Ask your credit card issuer for a chargeback, or sue in small claims court. The Ontario limit is $50,000.

How do I avoid a rogue mover?

Get at least three quotes, ask for an in-home estimate and a written contract, and avoid large cash deposits.
