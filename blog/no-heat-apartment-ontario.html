No Heat in Your Apartment in Ontario? What to Do Today

Published November 16, 2026 · Updated November 16, 2026 · 5 min read
By Kasım Demir · Founder, ComplaintCA

Short answer: tell your landlord in writing today. Ontario's minimum is 20°C from September 1 to June 15 when the landlord provides the heat, and Toronto's bylaw requires 21°C from October 1 to May 15. If nothing happens, call your city (311 in Toronto), then apply to the Landlord and Tenant Board (LTB) to get rent back.

What temperature must a landlord provide?

Other cities and townsCheck your municipality's property standards bylaw

Toronto's dates changed on April 30, 2025. Older articles still give September 15 to June 1. If your unit already reaches 21°C without heating, a Toronto landlord may turn the heat down or off.

The provincial 20°C rule is in O. Reg. 516/06. It does not apply to a unit where you control the temperature yourself and your main heat source can keep it at 20°C.

Heat is a vital service under the Residential Tenancies Act. A landlord cannot shut it off, even if you are behind on rent.

What to do today, in order

  1. Measure and record. The regulation measures 1.5 metres above the floor and 1 metre from outside walls, so put the thermometer there. Photograph the reading with the date and time, morning and evening.
  2. Tell the landlord or superintendent in writing. In Toronto, landlords must respond to urgent requests such as no heat within 24 hours.
  3. Call the city if it is not fixed. In Toronto, call 311 or file a service request for low or no heat. The City aims to contact you within 24 hours for urgent no-heat requests, and a bylaw officer can investigate. Elsewhere, ask your city or town hall for property standards or bylaw enforcement.
  4. Keep paying your rent. A landlord can apply to evict for unpaid rent even when the unit needs repairs. Claim the money back through the LTB instead.

A message that works:

“The temperature in unit [number] at [address] was [reading]°C at [time] on [date]. Please restore the heat to at least [20 or 21]°C today and tell me when the repair will be finished.”

If the landlord shut the heat off on purpose

Withholding or interfering with a vital service is an offence under the Residential Tenancies Act. That includes the case where the heat is cut because the landlord did not pay the utility bill.

Call the Rental Housing Enforcement Unit (RHEU) at 416-585-7214 or toll-free 1-888-772-9277, Monday to Friday, 8:30 a.m. to 5 p.m. You do not need to file an application. You must give your name: the unit does not take anonymous complaints.

The RHEU can contact the landlord and explain the law, and may open an investigation. A conviction can bring a fine of up to $100,000 for an individual and $500,000 for a corporation.

How to get rent back through the LTB

The city and the RHEU can push the landlord to fix the heat. Only the Landlord and Tenant Board can order money back to you.

FormUse it when
T6 (maintenance)The heating system is broken or not maintained
T2 (tenant rights)The landlord withheld or interfered with a vital service
  • Fee: $53 per application, or $48 through the Tribunals Ontario Portal. A fee waiver is available.
  • Deadline: file within one year of the problem.
  • What to ask for: a rent abatement for the cold days, an order to repair, and out-of-pocket costs such as a space heater you had to buy.

Hearings now take about 9 to 12 months or more, so call the city first and file with the LTB in parallel. Questions: LTB at 416-645-8080 or 1-888-332-3234. Full form steps are in T2 or T6: complaint against a landlord. Condo owner with a building-wide outage? Start with the condo complaint guide.

Frequently Asked

What temperature does a landlord have to keep an apartment in Ontario?

At least 20°C from September 1 to June 15 when the landlord provides the heat. Cities can set their own bylaw: Toronto requires 21°C from October 1 to May 15.

Who do I call if I have no heat in Toronto?

Tell your landlord in writing first. If it is not fixed, call 311 or file a service request. The City aims to contact you within 24 hours for urgent no-heat requests.

Can my landlord turn off the heat because I owe rent?

No. Heat is a vital service, and withholding it is an offence under the Residential Tenancies Act. Call the Rental Housing Enforcement Unit at 1-888-772-9277.

Can I stop paying rent until the heat is fixed?

No. A landlord can apply to evict for unpaid rent. Keep paying and file a T6 or T2 application with the Landlord and Tenant Board to ask for a rent abatement.

How much does it cost to apply to the LTB about no heat?

$53 per application, or $48 if you file through the Tribunals Ontario Portal. You can ask for a fee waiver if you cannot afford it.
