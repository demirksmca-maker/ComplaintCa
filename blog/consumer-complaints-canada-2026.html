Banking and financial services continue to dominate complaint volumes, with billing disputes and unauthorized charges accounting for over half of all complaints in this category. Telecommunications complaints have stabilized after the 61% spike reported in 2025, but still represent a significant portion of overall complaints. Retail and e-commerce complaints are rising sharply as more Canadians shop online, often encountering delayed refunds and unresponsive merchants.

Why Are Complaints Surging?

1. Post-Inflation Economic Pressure

Rising costs of living have made consumers more vigilant about billing errors and unexpected charges. Even small overages now trigger formal complaints. Households watching every dollar are far more likely to escalate disputes than in previous years.

2. Better Awareness of Consumer Rights

Digital channels and social media have made complaint processes more visible and accessible. Canadians increasingly know they have free, legal avenues to resolve disputes without hiring lawyers. This awareness is driving up complaint volumes across all sectors.

3. Service Quality Deterioration

Labor shortages in customer service have led to slower response times and reduced quality. When first-contact resolution fails, frustrated customers escalate through official ombudsman channels like CCTS.

How to File an Effective Complaint

If you're experiencing an issue with a bank, telecom provider, utility, or retailer, here's how to ensure your complaint gets resolved and escalated properly:

Step 1: Document Everything Thoroughly

Take screenshots of account statements, save all emails, and record dates and times of calls. Companies are significantly more likely to respond when you provide clear, timestamped evidence of the problem.

Step 2: Complain to the Company First (In Writing)

Most regulatory frameworks require you to attempt resolution with the company before escalating to ombudsman services. Send a formal written complaint (email counts) outlining the issue and what resolution you're seeking.

Step 3: Escalate to the Official Ombudsman

If the company doesn't respond within 30 days or refuses to resolve the issue, file a complaint with CCTS (for banking/telecom), your provincial ombudsman, or the relevant regulator. These services are free and their decisions are legally binding.

Step 4: Put Your Complaint in Plain Language

Don't assume the ombudsman understands industry jargon. Explain what happened, why it's a problem, and what outcome you want—in clear, simple language that anyone can follow.

What's Driving the Trend?

The 28% increase reflects a fundamental shift in consumer behavior. Canadians are no longer passively accepting service failures—they're actively seeking redress through official channels. This is healthy for the economy, as companies that ignore complaints are forced to improve or lose customers to competitors who prioritize service quality.

However, the surge also reveals systemic issues. If a bank receives hundreds of complaints about overdraft fees, that's a signal that the fee structure is unfair. If telecom companies generate thousands of billing complaints, that's a sign that billing processes are broken. The complaint data is, in essence, Canada's consumer report card.

What to Expect in 2027

As economic pressures persist and consumer awareness continues to grow, we expect complaint volumes to remain elevated through 2027. Companies that prioritize customer service will gain a competitive advantage in retention and reputation. Those that ignore complaints will face regulatory pressure and customer defection.
