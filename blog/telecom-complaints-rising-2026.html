October 2026 Update

The CCTS's preliminary numbers for its full 2025-26 year (August 2025 to July 2026) show a new record: 53,195 complaints received, up 79%, and 41,235 accepted, up 74%. It resolved 87.2% of concluded complaints. The CCTS warns that cases needing investigation are taking longer than usual because of the volume. The full annual report is due January 13, 2027.

What's Actually Happening

The Commission for Complaints for Telecom-television Services (CCTS) accepted 19,157 complaints between August 2025 and January 2026 — a 61% jump year over year. 56% of all complaints were about wireless service specifically, and billing remains the single largest category of complaint, ahead of service outages or contract disputes.

How the Free Process Actually Works

Step 1 — complain to your provider first. The CCTS requires you to give your provider a chance to resolve the issue, and to receive their final position (or wait a set period without response) before they'll accept your case.

Step 2 — file with the CCTS. It's free, and providers are contractually required to participate. Decisions can include bill credits, contract cancellation without penalty, or other remedies.

Get your complaint documented and structured before you contact your provider — it makes escalating to the CCTS faster if it comes to that.

Keeping evidence when the bill is the problem

Telecom disputes tend to be won on paper, not on memory. Download your bills for the months in dispute and highlight the line that is wrong. Ask for a copy of your contract and any promotion that was offered, and note the date and name each time you speak with the provider.

The CRTC's Wireless Code gives customers specific rights, among them a trial period for new wireless contracts and free unlocking of devices. Check the current text of the Code before relying on a detail, because it is reviewed from time to time. If your provider will not honour a promise, a written summary of what was promised and when is the most persuasive thing you can send to the CCTS.

Before you cancel in anger

Cancelling a service during a dispute can add fees or break a promotion. Ask your provider in writing what happens if you leave, note the answer, and consider filing with the CCTS first so the complaint is recorded while the service is still active.

Frequently Asked

How many complaints did the CCTS receive in 2025-26?

Preliminary numbers for August 2025 to July 2026 show a record 53,195 complaints received (up 79%) and 41,235 accepted (up 74%). The CCTS resolved 87.2% of concluded complaints.

Is the CCTS free, and do I have to complain to my provider first?

Yes to both. The CCTS is free and providers must participate. You must first give your provider a chance to resolve the issue and receive their final position, or wait the set period without a response.

What remedies can the CCTS give?

Decisions can include bill credits, contract cancellation without penalty, or other remedies.

Which providers get the most CCTS complaints?

Between August 2025 and January 2026: Rogers (including Shaw) 27%, Telus 21% and Bell 17%. Together they account for about two-thirds of accepted complaints.
