Insurance Claim Denied in Canada? How to Dispute It, Step by Step

Published October 5, 2026 · Updated November 3, 2026 · 7 min read

Short answer: get the denial in writing and ask which policy clause the insurer used, then use its internal complaint process and ask for a final position letter. With that letter, the General Insurance OmbudService (GIO) reviews home, auto and business disputes for free: 1-877-225-0446.

A denied claim is not the insurer's last word. Every insurer has an internal complaint process, and after it, an independent service reviews disputes for free. Most people skip the step that unlocks it: the final position letter.

Step by Step

1Get the denial in writing. Ask the insurer to name the exact policy clause it relied on. Keep the policy, photos, repair quotes and every email.

2Use the insurer's complaint process. Write to its complaints officer or internal ombudsman and explain why the clause doesn't apply. Ask for a final position letter at the end. You need it for the next step.

3Take it to GIO, for free. The General Insurance OmbudService reviews home, auto and business insurance disputes with its member insurers. It looks at the file and gives a non-binding recommendation. Call 1-877-225-0446 or apply at giocanada.org.

4Still not resolved? You can complain to your provincial insurance regulator about how the insurer treated you, or take the money dispute to court. For smaller amounts, see our small claims guide.

What to write in your complaint:

  • Your name, policy number and claim number.
  • The dates: loss or event, claim filed, insurer's decision.
  • What the insurer decided and the policy clause it named.
  • Why you disagree, in two or three plain sentences.
  • What you want: pay the claim, reverse the decision, or a written final answer.
  • Copies attached: policy, denial letter, photos, quotes, receipts and emails.

GIO can recommend a fix and review the file for free, but it cannot force your insurer to pay and does not review prices.

Car Insurance Claim Denied?

1Ask why, in writing. Car claims are often denied because of a policy exclusion, a late report, a driver not listed on the policy, or a dispute about who was at fault. Ask the insurer to name the clause or rule it used.

2Disagree with the fault decision? In Ontario, fault is set by the provincial Fault Determination Rules, not by the police report alone. Ask which rule the insurer applied and send any photos, dashcam video or witness details that show it got the facts wrong.

3Complain, then go to GIO. Use the insurer's complaint process, get the final position letter, then take private auto disputes to GIO for free.

4Public auto insurer? In BC, Manitoba, Saskatchewan and Quebec, use ICBC, MPI, SGI or SAAQ's own review process. Injury benefits in Ontario go to the Licence Appeal Tribunal.

Travel Insurance Claim Denied?

Travel medical claims follow the same path, but they are usually denied for different reasons, so the evidence you need is different. For the full appeal process, see travel insurance claim denied: how to appeal.

1Find the exact reason. The most common ones are a pre-existing condition that was not stable for the policy's stability period before you left (often 90, 180 or 365 days), an answer on the medical questionnaire the insurer says was wrong or incomplete, or a treatment the policy excludes. Ask the insurer to quote the clause.

2Ask for your claim file. Request a copy of your application and medical questionnaire, the denial letter, and the medical records the insurer used. Compare its definition of "stable" with what actually happened before your trip.

3Answer it with evidence. A letter from your own doctor saying the condition was stable, with no new symptoms, tests or medication changes in that period, is often the strongest document. Add itemized hospital bills and proof of travel dates.

4Complain in writing, then go to OLHI. Use the insurer's complaint process and ask for a final position letter. Then the OmbudService for Life & Health Insurance (OLHI) reviews it for free if your insurer is a participant. If the insurer hasn't answered after 90 days, OLHI can ask it about your complaint.

5Bought it through a credit card? The claim and the complaint go to the insurance company named in the card's certificate of insurance, not to the bank.

Not sure if it's OLHI or GIO? Most travel medical insurance goes to OLHI. Some travel policies, such as trip cancellation cover sold by a home and auto insurer, can be a GIO case. Your insurer's final position letter usually names the service to go to, so check the letter or ask the insurer. Filing with OLHI also pauses the legal limitation period until your complaint is closed, except in Quebec.
Ontario accident benefits have a hard deadline. You generally have 2 years from the insurer's denial of a specific benefit to apply to the Licence Appeal Tribunal. Each denial starts its own clock, so note the date on every refusal letter.

Explain why the clause doesn't apply, in a clear letter to your insurer.

Frequently Asked

Can I go straight to the General Insurance OmbudService?

No. You first need a final position letter from your insurer, which you get by completing its internal complaint process. Once you have it and still disagree, you can take the dispute to GIO for free.

Is GIO an insurance company?

No. In Canada, GIO is the General Insurance OmbudService, an independent and free dispute resolution service for home, auto and business insurance. It is not an insurer and does not sell policies.

Is a GIO decision binding on my insurer?

No. GIO reviews the file and gives a non-binding recommendation. Many disputes settle at that stage. If yours doesn't, you can still go to your provincial regulator or to court.

What if my Ontario accident benefits were denied?

Accident benefits disputes in Ontario go to the Licence Appeal Tribunal, not GIO. You generally have 2 years from the insurer's denial of a specific benefit to apply, and each denial has its own deadline.

What should I do if my travel insurance claim is denied?

Ask the insurer for the exact clause it relied on and a copy of your claim file, including the medical questionnaire you completed. Send a written complaint with your evidence, such as a doctor's letter, and ask for a final position letter. If you still disagree, take it to the free OmbudService for Life & Health Insurance (OLHI, 1-888-295-8112) if your insurer is a participant.

Why are travel insurance claims denied in Canada?

Common reasons are a pre-existing condition that was not stable for the policy's stability period before you left (often 90, 180 or 365 days), an answer on the medical questionnaire the insurer says was wrong or incomplete, or a treatment the policy excludes. Ask the insurer to point to the exact wording.

My travel insurance came with my credit card. Who do I complain to?

To the insurance company named in the card's certificate of insurance, not the bank. That insurer handles the claim and the internal complaint, and its final position letter is what you take to the OmbudService.

What should I do if my car insurance company denies my claim?

Ask the insurer to explain the denial in writing and name the clause or rule it used. Send evidence that answers it, use the insurer's complaint process, and ask for a final position letter. Then you can take a private auto dispute to the General Insurance OmbudService (GIO) for free.

Can I dispute an at-fault decision in Ontario?

Yes. Ontario insurers set fault using the provincial Fault Determination Rules. Ask which rule was applied, send photos, dashcam video or witness details if the facts are wrong, and use the insurer's complaint process. After its final position letter, you can go to GIO.

Can an insurance company deny a claim?

Yes, but only under the terms of your policy. The insurer has to tell you why and should be able to name the clause it relied on. If the facts don't fit that clause, use the insurer's complaint process, get a final position letter, and take it to the free ombudservice.

Who is the insurance ombudsman in Ontario?

For home, auto and business insurance, it is the General Insurance OmbudService (GIO, 1-877-225-0446). For life, health and travel medical insurance, it is the OmbudService for Life & Health Insurance (OLHI, 1-888-295-8112). Complaints about how an insurer treats customers can also go to the Financial Services Regulatory Authority of Ontario (FSRA).

Why do home insurance claims get denied?

Common reasons are a policy exclusion such as wear and tear, a slow leak or flood, reporting the loss late, an answer on the application the insurer says was wrong, not enough proof of the loss, or the home being vacant longer than the policy allows.
