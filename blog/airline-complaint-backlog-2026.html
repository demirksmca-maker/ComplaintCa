It Now Takes 987 Days to Resolve an Airline Complaint in Canada

Published August 30, 2026 · Updated November 3, 2026 · 3 min read

Short answer: complain to the airline first, because the Canadian Transportation Agency (CTA) will not accept your complaint until you have. The CTA's average resolution time is now about 987 days, with a backlog of more than 95,000 complaints, so plan for a wait of two years or more.

If your flight was delayed, cancelled, or you were bumped and the airline hasn't made it right, the official complaint process exists — but the backlog behind it means "official" doesn't mean fast.

October 2026 Update

The CTA's own figures put the backlog at more than 95,000 complaints at the end of its 2025-26 fiscal year (March 31, 2026), and about 97,000 by May, with over 1,000 new complaints arriving every week. Ottawa says it will bring in an independent third-party dispute resolution body to help clear the queue. Until that is running, plan for a wait of two years or more.

What's Actually Happening

The Canadian Transportation Agency is sitting on a backlog of roughly 97,000 air travel complaints. January 2026 set a new record with 5,685 complaints filed in a single month. The federal transport minister has publicly committed to clearing the backlog and has increased fines airlines face for non-compliance — but none of that changes the wait for a complaint already in the queue.

The Required First Step

The CTA will not accept your complaint until you've given the airline itself a chance to resolve it first. That means:

  • File directly with the airline through its own complaint process
  • Give them a reasonable window to respond (their policy will state a timeframe)
  • Only then does an unresolved complaint become eligible for the CTA

What This Means If You're Filing Now

Don't wait to document. Boarding passes, receipts for meals or hotels caused by the delay, and the airline's own written response all matter more the longer the file sits.

If it's purely a refund dispute and you paid by credit card, a chargeback can move faster than a CTA complaint — see our chargeback guide for how that process works and its own deadlines.

A CTA complaint is still worth filing even with the wait — it's the official record, and compensation under Air Passenger Protection Regulations is often owed regardless of how long the file takes to process.

Start your documented complaint now — the CTA backlog doesn't reset the clock on your case.

What to put in a CTA complaint so it is not slowed down

With a queue this long, an incomplete file loses time twice. Before filing with the CTA, gather the booking confirmation, boarding passes, the airline's written reply and receipts for expenses caused by the disruption. State which rule you rely on (a delay, a cancellation or denied boarding) and the amount or remedy you want.

Keep copies of every message to the airline. If you reached the point where the airline had 30 days to answer and did not, say so and attach the date of your claim. If your only goal is a refund of money you paid by card, a chargeback may be quicker than waiting for a CTA decision.

How long you have to start

Under the Air Passenger Protection Regulations you generally have one year from the disruption to claim from the airline. Put that date in your calendar. The clock runs while the CTA queue moves, so the claim to the airline is the step to protect first.

Frequently Asked

How long does a CTA air travel complaint take?

The Canadian Transportation Agency's average time to resolve an air travel complaint is now about 987 days. The backlog was more than 95,000 complaints at the end of its 2025-26 fiscal year, so plan for a wait of two years or more.

Do I have to complain to the airline first?

Yes. The CTA will not accept your complaint until you have given the airline a chance to resolve it through its own complaint process.

How long do I have to claim from the airline?

Under the Air Passenger Protection Regulations you generally have one year from the disruption to claim from the airline. Protect that date first.

Is a chargeback faster than a CTA complaint?

If it is only a refund dispute and you paid by credit card, a chargeback can be faster than waiting for a CTA decision. Card networks set their own deadlines, so check them early.
