How to Recover an Unpaid Invoice in Canada: Demand Letter, Deadlines and Small Claims

Published October 5, 2026 · Updated November 3, 2026 · 5 min read

A client who stops answering your emails is not the end of the road. Most unpaid invoices are recovered with a firm letter, and the rest can go to small claims court without a lawyer. The order matters, and so does the deadline.

Step by Step

1Collect your paper trail. Gather the invoice, the quote or contract, the emails where the client approved the work, and proof of delivery. Courts decide these cases on documents.

2Send a demand letter. State the amount, the invoice numbers and dates, and a clear deadline, usually 10 days. Say what you will do if it isn't paid. Almost every small claims court expects you to have asked in writing first, and you'll attach this letter to your claim.

3Add interest the right way. You can charge interest only if your terms said so. Under the federal Interest Act, a monthly rate with no yearly equivalent is capped at 5% a year. Write it as "1.5% per month (18% per year)".

4Check the deadline. The 2-year clock usually starts when payment was due and you knew it wasn't coming. In Ontario, a signed written acknowledgment of the debt, or a partial payment, restarts the clock if it comes before the deadline.

5File in small claims court. No lawyer is needed. In Ontario, the defendant has 20 days to file a defence after being served. If they don't, you can ask for a default judgment.

Demand Letter: Sample Wording

"Our records show that invoice #[number], dated [date], for $[amount], is unpaid. It was due on [due date]. Please pay the full amount, plus interest of [rate] as set out in our terms, by [date, about 10 days from now]. If we do not receive payment by then, we will file a claim in small claims court without further notice, and ask for interest and court costs."

Send it by email and by a method with proof of delivery, such as registered mail. Keep it polite and factual. A judge may read it later.

Frequently Asked

How long do I have to sue over an unpaid invoice in Canada?

In Ontario, BC, Alberta, Saskatchewan and Manitoba, the basic limitation period is 2 years from when you knew, or should have known, about the claim. In Quebec it is 3 years. In Ontario, a written and signed acknowledgment of the debt, or a partial payment, restarts the 2-year clock if it happens before the deadline.

Can I charge interest on an overdue invoice?

Yes, if your terms say so. But under section 4 of the federal Interest Act, if you state interest as a monthly or daily rate without also stating the yearly equivalent, you can only recover 5% a year. Write it as, for example, "1.5% per month (18% per year)".

How much does it cost to file a small claim for an unpaid invoice in Ontario?

Filing a Plaintiff's Claim costs $108 if you file fewer than 10 claims a year at that court, and $228 if you are a frequent claimant. Ontario's Small Claims Court handles claims up to $50,000, not counting interest and costs.

Can a business use small claims court in Quebec?

Only smaller ones. In Quebec, a company can sue in the Small Claims Division only if it had no more than 10 employees in the 12 months before filing. The limit is $15,000, and lawyers cannot represent you at the hearing.

What can I do if a client won't pay an invoice?

Gather the invoice, the contract or quote, and the emails approving the work. Send a written demand letter with a firm deadline, usually 10 days. If the client still doesn't pay, file a claim in small claims court. No lawyer is needed, and in most provinces you have 2 years to sue.

Should I send a demand letter before going to small claims court?

Yes. It often gets you paid without going to court, and courts expect you to have asked in writing first. State the amount, the invoice numbers and dates, the deadline, and that you will file a claim if it isn't paid.
