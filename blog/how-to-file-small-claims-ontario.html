How to File a Small Claims Case in Ontario, Step by Step

Published October 3, 2026 · Updated November 3, 2026 · 7 min read

Short answer: fill out the Plaintiff's Claim (Form 7A), file it with the court and pay the fee, then serve the defendant within six months of the court stamping it. The defendant has 20 days after being served to file a defence.

Ontario's Small Claims Court is designed for people without lawyers. Here's the whole process — the form, the fees, and every deadline — from your demand letter to trial.

Before You Start

  • Limit: $50,000 or less, not counting interest and costs (up from $35,000 since October 1, 2025).
  • Deadline: in most cases, you can't file more than two years after the incident.
  • Lawyer? Not required. You can hire a lawyer or paralegal if you want help.
  • Demand letter: ask the other side to pay first, in writing, with a clear deadline. Keep a copy.

The 6 Steps

1Fill out the Plaintiff's Claim (Form 7A). Say who you're suing, how much you want, and why. Attach your key documents.

2File it. You can file online if you're eligible, or in person or by mail at the courthouse. Pay the filing fee.

3Serve the defendant. After the court stamps your claim, you have six months to serve it on the person or business you're suing.

4Wait 20 days. The defendant has 20 days after being served to file a defence. If they don't, and the amount owed is clear, you can ask for a default judgment.

5Settlement conference. If the defendant disputes the claim, you meet with a judge to try to settle and narrow the issues.

6Trial. If there's no settlement, you set a trial date and present your case.

Most cases never reach trial. Many settle after the demand letter, at the settlement conference, or because the defendant never files a defence. Ask your courthouse about current wait times when you file.

Filing Online

Ontario lets you file many Small Claims Court documents online through Justice Services Online, including a Plaintiff's Claim, a Defence and a request for default judgment. You pay the fee by card and get the issued claim back by email, so you don't have to stand in line at the courthouse.

Some steps still need paper or an in-person visit, and some claims can't be filed online. If the portal won't accept your claim, file it at the courthouse or by mail instead.

What to Bring as Evidence

  • The deal: contract, quote, invoice, receipt or written agreement.
  • The problem: photos, videos, repair estimates or expert reports.
  • The messages: emails, texts and your demand letter, with dates.
  • The money: bank records or cheques showing what was paid or owed.
  • Witnesses: names and contact details of anyone who saw what happened.

Where to File

File in the Small Claims Court location for the area where the problem happened, or where the defendant lives or does business. For example, a dispute with a car dealer in Pickering goes to the court that serves Durham Region. In Toronto, the Small Claims Court is at 47 Sheppard Avenue East, 3rd floor (Yonge and Sheppard), phone 416-326-3554.

If you're not sure, call the courthouse before you file. Filing in the wrong place can mean the case is moved, which adds delay.

Can You Get Your Costs Back?

Often, yes. The judge can order the losing side to pay part of your costs. Under section 29 of the Courts of Justice Act, a costs award in Small Claims Court, not counting disbursements such as court fees, cannot exceed 15% of the amount claimed, unless the court is penalizing unreasonable behaviour.

You Won. Now Get Paid

A judgment doesn't pay itself. If the other side doesn't pay, you have to enforce it:

1Examination hearing. The debtor must come to court and answer questions under oath about their income, bank accounts and property.

2Garnishment. Take money directly from their wages or bank account.

3Writ of seizure and sale. Have the sheriff seize and sell their property, or register it against land they own.

Can't afford the fees? Ask for a fee waiver. You may qualify if your main household income is Ontario Works, ODSP, OAS with GIS, War Veterans Allowance or CPP, or if your gross household income is under the limit, for example $33,100 for one person or $68,700 for four, with liquid assets under $2,800 and net worth under $11,100. Use form FW-A-3, or FW-A-4 if you don't meet the limits but still can't pay. Businesses can't apply.
Outside Ontario? Limits run from $15,000 in Quebec to $100,000 in Alberta. See the 2026 small claims limits table and our small claims guide.

Most claims start with a demand letter. Get yours written clearly, with the amount and a deadline, before you file.

Frequently Asked

How much does it cost to file small claims in Ontario?

Filing a Plaintiff's Claim costs $108 for an infrequent claimant and $228 for a frequent claimant. Setting a trial date costs $308 (infrequent) or $403 (frequent). A fee waiver is available if you can't afford the fees.

What is the small claims limit in Ontario?

$50,000, not including interest and costs. It rose from $35,000 on October 1, 2025.

Do I need a lawyer for small claims court in Ontario?

No. You don't need a lawyer or paralegal to sue someone in Small Claims Court, though you can hire one for help.

How long does the defendant have to respond to a small claim in Ontario?

20 days after being served with your claim. If they don't file a defence and the amount owed is clear, you can request a default judgment.

Can I get my court fees back if I win small claims in Ontario?

Often, yes. The judge can order the losing side to pay your costs. Under section 29 of the Courts of Justice Act, a costs award in Small Claims Court, not counting disbursements such as court fees, cannot exceed 15% of the amount claimed, unless the court is penalizing unreasonable behaviour.

Who qualifies for a court fee waiver in Ontario?

You may qualify if your main household income is Ontario Works, ODSP, OAS with GIS, War Veterans Allowance or CPP, or if your gross household income is below the limit for your household size, such as $33,100 for one person, with liquid assets under $2,800 and net worth under $11,100. Businesses cannot get a fee waiver.

How do I file for small claims court in Ontario?

Fill out a Plaintiff's Claim (Form 7A), then file it online if you're eligible, or in person or by mail at the courthouse, and pay the $108 filing fee (infrequent claimant). Serve the stamped claim on the defendant within six months. The defendant then has 20 days to file a defence.

Where do I file a small claim in Ontario?

In the Small Claims Court location for the area where the problem happened, or where the defendant lives or carries on business. If you're not sure which one applies, call the courthouse before filing.

Where is the Toronto Small Claims Court?

The Toronto Small Claims Court is at 47 Sheppard Avenue East, 3rd floor, near Yonge and Sheppard. The phone number is 416-326-3554. Many claims can also be filed online, so you may not need to go in person.

How long does small claims court take in Ontario?

It depends on the case. If the defendant doesn't file a defence within 20 days of service, you can ask for a default judgment, often within weeks. If they defend, there is a settlement conference, usually several months later, and a trial can take a year or more from filing, longer in busy courts like Toronto. Most cases settle before trial.

Can I file a small claim online in Ontario?

Yes, in many cases. Ontario's Justice Services Online lets you file a Plaintiff's Claim, a Defence and some other documents, and pay the fee by card. If your claim can't be filed online, file it at the courthouse or by mail.

What happens if I win small claims court but don't get paid?

You have to enforce the judgment. You can hold an examination hearing to find out what the debtor owns, garnish their wages or bank account, or get a writ of seizure and sale. Each step has its own court fee, such as $144 for a garnishment notice.
