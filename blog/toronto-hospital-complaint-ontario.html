Hospital Complaint in Toronto? Here Are the 3 Steps, in Order

Published November 3, 2026 · Updated November 3, 2026 · 3 min read

Short answer: write to the hospital's Patient Relations office first. If that does not fix it, go to Ontario's Patient Ombudsman at 1-888-321-0339. For a doctor's conduct, file with the College of Physicians and Surgeons of Ontario (CPSO).

Step 1: Write to the hospital

Every Toronto hospital has a Patient Relations office or a similar team. Find it on the hospital's own website. Send your complaint in writing, so you have a dated copy.

Say who was involved, which unit, and the dates. Say what you want: an explanation, a review, a meeting or a change in practice.

Step 2: Patient Ombudsman

If the hospital does not resolve it, escalate to Ontario's Patient Ombudsman. The office reviews hospitals, long-term care homes, home care and community surgical and diagnostic centres.

Phone: 1-888-321-0339. Keep your letter to the hospital and its reply. They show you tried the hospital first.

↑ Back to sections

Step 3: A doctor or a nurse

The hospital and its doctors are separate. A complaint about one doctor's conduct goes to the CPSO. A complaint about a nurse goes to the College of Nurses of Ontario.

See our step-by-step guide: How to file a complaint about a doctor with the CPSO.

What to put in the letter

  • Who and where. Patient name, hospital, unit, dates of the visit or stay.
  • What happened. Short facts, in order. Names or roles of staff if you know them.
  • What you want. One clear request.
  • Proof. Discharge papers, test results, your notes, copies of emails.

Keep a copy of everything you send.

Frequently Asked

Who do I complain to about a Toronto hospital?

First the hospital's Patient Relations office, in writing. If it does not resolve the problem, Ontario's Patient Ombudsman at 1-888-321-0339.

Can I complain about a doctor and the hospital at the same time?

Yes, they are separate. The hospital handles care and service. The College of Physicians and Surgeons of Ontario handles a doctor's conduct.

Who handles a complaint about a nurse?

The College of Nurses of Ontario, not the CPSO. The CPSO only covers licensed doctors.

Can a family member complain for the patient?

If the patient cannot write because of illness, a family member or support person can usually complain on their behalf with the patient's consent.
