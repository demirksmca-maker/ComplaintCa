How Much Does Small Claims Court Cost in Ontario? Every Fee for 2026

Published October 7, 2026 · 5 min read
$416
in court fees to file a claim ($108) and set a trial date ($308) in Ontario Small Claims Court, for most people. Many cases settle before the trial fee is due.

Small claims court is the cheapest way to sue in Ontario, and you don't need a lawyer. But the fees come in stages, and some are paid only if the case goes further. Here is every fee, when you pay it, and how much you can get back if you win.

The Main Fees

StepFee
File a Plaintiff's Claim$108
File a Defence (the person being sued)$77
File a Defendant's Claim$108
Request a default judgment (no defence filed)$94
File a motion$127
Summons to a witness$33
Fix a date for trial$308

These are the fees for most people. If you have already filed 10 or more claims this year at the same court office, you are a frequent claimant and pay more: $228 to file a claim, $128 for a default judgment and $403 to fix a trial date.

Step by Step: What You Pay and When

1When you file: $108. You pay this to start the case. You can file online or at the court office.

2If they don't answer: $94. The defendant usually has 20 days to file a defence after being served. If they don't, you can ask for a default judgment.

3If they defend: settlement conference. Most defended cases go to a settlement conference first, where many settle.

4If it doesn't settle: $308. You pay this to fix a trial date.

5If you win but aren't paid: enforcement fees. A garnishment notice costs $144, a writ of seizure and sale $68, and a certificate of judgment $30.

Two Real Examples

Your claimFees to trial / most you can recover in costs
$5,000$416 / up to $750
$35,000$416 / up to $5,250

Court fees are the same whatever you claim. The "costs" a judge can order the loser to pay are capped at 15% of the amount claimed, not counting disbursements such as court fees, unless the court is penalizing unreasonable behaviour.

Can't afford the fees? You can ask for a fee waiver. You may qualify if your main household income is Ontario Works, ODSP, OAS with GIS, War Veterans Allowance or CPP, or if your gross household income is under the limit, for example $33,100 for one person or $68,700 for four, with liquid assets under $2,800 and net worth under $11,100. Use form FW-A-3, or FW-A-4 if you don't meet the limits but still can't pay. Businesses can't apply.

The limit in Ontario Small Claims Court is $50,000 since October 1, 2025. In most cases you must sue within 2 years. Our step-by-step guide shows how to file a small claim in Ontario.

Before you file, send a demand letter. It often gets you paid without going to court.

Frequently Asked

How much does it cost to file a small claim in Ontario?

Filing a Plaintiff's Claim costs $108 if you file fewer than 10 claims a year at that court office, and $228 if you are a frequent claimant. If the case goes to trial, fixing a trial date costs $308, or $403 for a frequent claimant.

Can I get my court fees back if I win?

Often, yes. The judge can order the losing side to pay your costs. Under section 29 of the Courts of Justice Act, a costs award in Small Claims Court, not counting disbursements such as court fees, cannot exceed 15% of the amount claimed, unless the court is penalizing unreasonable behaviour.

Who qualifies for a court fee waiver in Ontario?

You may qualify if your main household income is Ontario Works, ODSP, OAS with GIS, War Veterans Allowance or CPP, or if your gross household income is below the limit for your household size, such as $33,100 for one person, with liquid assets under $2,800 and net worth under $11,100. Businesses cannot get a fee waiver.

What is the maximum I can sue for in Ontario Small Claims Court?

$50,000, since October 1, 2025. Before that date the limit was $35,000. In most cases you must sue within 2 years of the problem.
