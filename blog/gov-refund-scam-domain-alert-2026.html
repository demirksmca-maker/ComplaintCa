While reviewing what people search for right before landing on ComplaintCA, one pattern stood out: a cluster of searches trying to verify a single domain — "gov-refund.com" — using forensic tools like urlscan.io, VirusTotal, and WHOIS lookups. We checked it ourselves.

What We Found

A domain reputation check on gov-refund.com returned a suspicious verdict. The single biggest red flag: the domain was registered only one day before we checked it. A "government refund" site that's brand new is a textbook pattern — a real government agency's domain has existed for years; a scam site built to catch one news cycle or one wave of texts often goes live the same day the campaign starts.

How to Check Any Domain Like This Yourself

  • Domain age — a WHOIS lookup (whois.com) shows the registration date. New registration, especially under 30 days, is the single strongest signal for this kind of scam.
  • urlscan.io — shows you a live screenshot and the actual page structure without visiting it yourself.
  • VirusTotal — checks the domain against dozens of security vendors' blocklists at once.

None of these require an account, and none of them are the government agency the site is impersonating — that's the point. Never use a link or number the suspicious message gives you to "verify" itself.

If You've Already Interacted With It

If you entered any personal or banking information, contact your bank immediately, then report it to the Canadian Anti-Fraud Centre at 1-888-495-8501. Don't wait to see if anything happens first.
