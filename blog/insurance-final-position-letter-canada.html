Insurance Final Position Letter: How to Get One in Canada

Published October 16, 2026 · Updated November 3, 2026 · 4 min read

Short answer: a Final Position Letter is your insurer's written final answer after its internal complaint process is finished. The General Insurance OmbudService (GIO) cannot review a home, auto or business complaint until you have it, so ask the insurer for it in writing.

Your insurer turned down your home, auto or commercial claim, and you want an outside review. The free General Insurance OmbudService (GIO) can help, but only after the insurer gives you its Final Position Letter. Here is how to get it fast.

What It Is

A Final Position Letter is the insurer's written, final answer to your complaint. It arrives when the insurer has finished handling your complaint internally, normally signed by a senior complaints contact. It should say:

  • The decision on your complaint and the reasons.
  • That this is its final position.
  • Where to go next, usually GIO.

A claim denial from an adjuster is not the same thing. It is only the first answer.

How to Get It

1Find the complaint process. It is on the insurer's website, usually under "Complaints" or "Dispute resolution". Note the name or email of the complaint officer.

2Complain in writing. Give your policy and claim numbers, what happened, and what you want. Our complaint form can draft it.

3Go up each level. If the first answer is no, ask to take it to the next level, through each internal level until the insurer names its last one.

4Ask for the letter by name. Write: "If you will not change your decision, please send me your Final Position Letter."

5Take it to GIO. Forward the letter to GIO, along with the policy number and your email trail.

Sample Request

Subject: Request for Final Position Letter – Claim [number]

I complained about claim [number] on [date]. The problem is not solved. If your decision is final, please send me your Final Position Letter in writing, so I can take my complaint to the General Insurance OmbudService.

[Your name] · Policy [number] · [phone]

If the Letter Doesn't Come

  • Follow up in writing and keep every email and date.
  • Check the insurer's complaint policy for the response time it promises.
  • Call GIO at 1-877-225-0446. Its staff can guide you through the insurer's process, even before you have the letter.
  • Suspect misconduct? A separate complaint can go to the provincial insurance regulator (FSRA in Ontario).
Life or health insurance? The letter works the same way, but the outside body is the OmbudService for Life & Health Insurance (OLHI), 1-888-295-8112, not GIO.

Frequently Asked

What is a Final Position Letter?

It is your insurance company's written final answer to your complaint, after its internal complaint process is finished. It says the insurer's decision and points you to an outside body, such as GIO, if you still disagree.

Why do I need it?

GIO cannot review a home, auto or business insurance complaint until you have the insurer's Final Position Letter. It shows you finished the insurer's own process first.

How do I get one?

Complain in writing, follow the insurer's complaint steps up to its complaint officer or internal ombudsman, and ask clearly for a Final Position Letter if they still say no.

What if the insurer never sends it?

Ask again in writing with your claim and complaint numbers, and keep copies. GIO staff can talk with you and guide you through your insurer's complaint process even before the letter arrives.

Is a phone call enough?

Rarely. Put your complaint and your request in writing, by email or letter, so there is a dated record GIO can see.

Reading the letter when it arrives

Check three things as soon as you get it. First, does it say clearly that it is the insurer's final position? Second, does it explain the reasons for the decision? Third, does it name the outside body you can go to and any time limits that apply?

Also keep an eye on legal deadlines. In Ontario the basic limitation period for most civil claims is two years under the Limitations Act, 2002, and some insurance policies contain shorter contractual deadlines. Read your own policy and, for a large claim, speak to a lawyer before time runs out.

Keeping your own record in step

Create one folder, paper or digital, with the policy, the claim number, every letter and a log of calls with dates. When you ask for the Final Position Letter, refer to those dates so the insurer cannot say it has not seen your earlier complaint.
