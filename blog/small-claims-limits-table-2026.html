Small Claims Court Limits Just Jumped in 5 Provinces — Full 2026 Table

Published September 11, 2026 · 3 min read
$100,000
Alberta's small claims limit — the highest in Canada, and more than 6x what it was a decade ago.

If you checked your province's small claims limit even a year or two ago, the number you remember is probably out of date. Five provinces have raised theirs recently.

What's Actually Happening

Provincial small claims limits have moved sharply upward in the past three years, mostly to keep pace with inflation and reduce pressure on higher courts. Ontario's jump is the most recent — from $35,000 to $50,000, effective October 1, 2025.

What Changed Recently

  • Alberta — raised to $100,000 in August 2023, from $50,000.
  • Saskatchewan — raised to $50,000 effective April 1, 2024.
  • Manitoba — raised to $20,000 effective January 1, 2025.
  • Ontario — raised to $50,000 effective October 1, 2025, from $35,000.

If Your Claim Is Above the Limit

You can still file in small claims court for the maximum amount and formally abandon the excess, or proceed in a higher court instead. Either way, most courts expect a written demand letter to the other side first.
