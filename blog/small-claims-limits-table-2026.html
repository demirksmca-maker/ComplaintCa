October 2026 Update

We re-checked the limits in October 2026. No province has announced a new increase since Ontario's move to $50,000 on October 1, 2025, so the table below is still current. For the Atlantic provinces: Nova Scotia's limit is $25,000 and New Brunswick's is $20,000.

What's Actually Happening

Provincial small claims limits have moved sharply upward in the past three years, mostly to keep pace with inflation and reduce pressure on higher courts. Ontario's jump is the most recent — from $35,000 to $50,000, effective October 1, 2025.

What Changed Recently

  • Alberta — raised to $100,000 in August 2023, from $50,000.
  • Saskatchewan — raised to $50,000 effective April 1, 2024.
  • Manitoba — raised to $20,000 effective January 1, 2025.
  • Ontario — raised to $50,000 effective October 1, 2025, from $35,000.

If Your Claim Is Above the Limit

You can still file in small claims court for the maximum amount and formally abandon the excess, or proceed in a higher court instead. Either way, most courts expect a written demand letter to the other side first.

Choosing the right court for your amount

The limit is only one factor. For a debt, consider how likely the other side is to pay and whether you can prove the amount. Court fees and the time to prepare are modest compared with a higher court, but you still need clear documents: the contract, invoices, messages and proof of what you asked for.

Send a written demand letter before you file and keep the date. If your claim is slightly above the limit, many people choose to abandon the excess so the case can stay in small claims, but that choice is permanent, so think about it before you file.

Frequently Asked

What is the small claims limit in each province in 2026?

Alberta $100,000, Ontario $50,000, Saskatchewan $50,000, British Columbia $5,000 (Civil Resolution Tribunal) or $5,001 to $35,000 (Provincial Court), Manitoba $20,000, Quebec $15,000. Nova Scotia is $25,000 and New Brunswick is $20,000.

Which provinces raised their small claims limit recently?

Alberta (to $100,000 in August 2023), Saskatchewan (to $50,000 from April 1, 2024), Manitoba (to $20,000 from January 1, 2025) and Ontario (to $50,000 from October 1, 2025, up from $35,000).

What if my claim is above the small claims limit?

You can file in small claims court for the maximum amount and formally abandon the excess, or proceed in a higher court. Abandoning the excess is permanent.

Do I need a demand letter before filing?

Most courts expect a written demand letter to the other side first. Send it before you file and keep the date.
