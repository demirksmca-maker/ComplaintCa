GIO Canada: How to Complain to the General Insurance OmbudService

Published October 16, 2026 · Updated November 3, 2026 · 5 min read

Short answer: the General Insurance OmbudService (GIO) is a free, independent service for home, auto or business insurance disputes, in English and French. You can't go straight to it: first finish your insurer's own complaint process and get its Final Position Letter.

GIO is the place to turn when your home, car or business insurer won't fix a problem. It is independent from the insurance companies and doesn't cost you anything. But it has one rule that trips many people up: you must finish your insurer's own complaint process first.

What GIO Handles

  • Claims: a claim was denied, delayed or underpaid.
  • Coverage: you and the insurer read your policy differently.
  • Policy handling: how your policy was processed or managed, for example a cancellation or a billing mistake.

GIO does not review the price of insurance or rate increases.

Step by Step

1Complain to your insurer in writing. Insurers run an in-house complaint procedure, and the last stop is frequently a complaints officer. Say what happened, what you want, and keep copies. Our complaint form can draft the letter for you.

2Get the Final Position Letter. This is the insurer's written final answer. GIO needs it before it can review your complaint. See how to get one.

3File with GIO. Use the online form, call 1-877-225-0446 or email info@giocanada.org. Attach the Final Position Letter, the policy number and the correspondence you have sent.

4Consumer Service Officer. A GIO officer looks into the complaint first and tries to help you and the insurer reach a solution.

5Mediation, then Senior Adjudication. If it isn't solved, the complaint can go to mediation and then to Senior Adjudication, which gives a non-binding recommendation.

What to include when you file with GIO:

  • Your name, policy and claim number.
  • The dates: loss or event, claim filed, insurer's decision.
  • What the insurer decided and the policy clause it named.
  • Why you disagree, in two or three plain sentences.
  • What you want: pay the claim, reverse the decision, or a written final answer.
  • Attached: Final Position Letter, policy, denial letter and your emails with the insurer.

GIO can investigate and recommend a solution for free; it cannot force the insurer to accept it, and it does not review prices.

If GIO Doesn't Solve It

A GIO recommendation is not binding, so you keep your other options. For a money dispute, small claims court may fit. If you believe the insurer broke the rules, a separate complaint can go to your provincial insurance regulator, for example FSRA in Ontario. For a large claim, talk to a lawyer before any legal deadline passes.

Frequently Asked

What is GIO in Canada?

The General Insurance OmbudService (GIO) is an independent, free dispute-resolution service for people with home, auto or business insurance from one of its member insurance companies. It works in English and French.

Can I go straight to GIO?

No. You must first try to resolve the problem with your insurance company and get its Final Position Letter. If you are still unhappy after that letter, you can take the complaint to GIO.

What does GIO handle?

Disputes about claims, how your policy's coverage is interpreted, and how your policy was processed or handled. It does not review the price of insurance or rates.

Is a GIO decision binding?

No. If a complaint reaches GIO's Senior Adjudication stage, the result is a non-binding recommendation. You keep your other options, such as small claims court.

What about life or health insurance?

Those go to a different free body, the OmbudService for Life & Health Insurance (OLHI), at 1-888-295-8112.

Making your GIO file easy to read

A complaint is reviewed by a person who has never heard of your claim, so structure matters. Start with a two-sentence summary of what you asked the insurer to do and what it decided. Follow with a dated list of events, then attach the policy, the denial letter and the Final Position Letter.

Common sticking points are late notice of a claim, disputes about what the policy excludes, and different views on the value of repairs. State plainly which of these applies to you and point to the policy wording you rely on. Where you disagree on cost, include written estimates.

GIO or the regulator?

GIO helps resolve a dispute about your own claim or policy. A provincial regulator such as FSRA looks at whether an insurer followed the rules and generally does not decide an individual claim. If you believe the insurer acted improperly, you can contact both, but file with GIO for help with the dispute itself.
