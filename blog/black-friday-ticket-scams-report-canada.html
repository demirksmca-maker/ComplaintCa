Black Friday & Ticket Scams: Where to Report a Fraudulent Seller in Canada

Published October 19, 2026 · 5 min read
1-888-495-8501
Canadian Anti-Fraud Centre toll-free line

Holiday shopping season is when fake online stores, resale-ticket sellers and gift-card requests show up most. If you already paid, the order of your calls matters: the sooner your bank and the Canadian Anti-Fraud Centre hear about it, the better your chances of recovery.

Red Flags Before You Pay

The Government of Canada's holiday scams campaign highlights a few patterns worth knowing:

  • Fake or unfamiliar retailers. Buy from retailers that are genuine and research a seller before you pay. Counterfeit or inferior goods can also be a health risk.
  • Gift cards as payment. Legitimate businesses and organizations will not request gift cards as payment. Treat that request as a warning sign.
  • Tampered or resold gift cards. Check in-store cards for tampering, and buy online gift cards from the business directly, not from resale or auction sites.
  • Too-good-to-be-true prices. A deep discount, plus pressure to pay immediately and only by e-transfer, crypto or gift card, is the classic pattern for ticket and marketplace scams.

If You Already Paid: 6 Steps in Order

  1. Collect everything. Names, phone numbers, website address, screenshots, receipts and chat messages. Do not delete the conversation.
  2. Call your local police and keep the file number. The Competition Bureau says it is especially important to call police if you have lost money.
  3. Report to the Canadian Anti-Fraud Centre (CAFC) online or at 1-888-495-8501.
  4. Tell the bank, credit union, card company or payment provider that handled the money. Do this as early as possible.
  5. Report it on the platform where it happened (marketplace, social media or classified site) using its report-abuse or report-ad link.
  6. Ask Equifax and TransUnion for a fraud alert and a free credit report if you shared personal details.

Beware of "Recovery" Offers

After you report, you may be contacted by someone who claims they can recover your money for a fee. The Government of Canada identifies these offers as scams too. Real recovery goes through your bank, card issuer and police, not through a stranger who messages you.

Frequently Asked

Should I report a scam if I only lost a small amount?

Yes. Reports help police and the CAFC spot patterns, and your bank can only act if you tell it.

Who do I call first?

If you sent money, start with your bank or payment provider and local police, then report to the CAFC. Do all three on the same day if you can.

Can I get my money back?

It depends on how you paid and how fast you act. Acting quickly improves the chances, which is why the first call should be to the institution that moved the money.

What if the seller is a real business that just did not deliver?

That is usually a consumer dispute rather than a fraud. Start with a written complaint to the seller, then your card issuer, and see the related guides below.
