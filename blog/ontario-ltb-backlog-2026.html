Ontario's LTB Backlog Dropped by 12,000 Cases — But Your Wait Is Still 3x Longer

Published August 30, 2026 · Updated October 21, 2026 · 4 min read
41,465
cases still backlogged at Ontario's Landlord and Tenant Board — down from 53,057, but still a long line.

Good news and bad news from the LTB's latest numbers: the backlog is genuinely shrinking. The bad news is "shrinking" still means most people are waiting months — sometimes close to a year — for a hearing.

October 2026 Update

Tribunals Ontario set a goal of minimal or no LTB backlog by the end of the 2025-26 fiscal year. Hearing waits reported in spring 2026 were about 3 months for non-payment applications (L1/L9) and roughly 5 to 7 months for most others. Complaints about the LTB to the Ontario Ombudsman fell to 779 in 2025-26, from 971 the year before. Tenant applications can still take longer, so keep documenting while you wait.

What's Actually Happening

Ontario's Landlord and Tenant Board has reduced its backlog from 53,057 cases to 41,465 — a real drop of roughly 12,000 cases, and the Board received a record number of new applications while doing it. That's a genuine improvement.

But context matters: landlords and tenants are still waiting about three times as long for a hearing as they were before the LTB came under Tribunals Ontario's management. A faster backlog reduction doesn't mean a fast process — it means a slightly less slow one.

Across all case types, 80% of hearings are scheduled somewhere between 3.2 and 14.1 months out — and last-minute adjournments can push the actual resolution later still.

What This Means If You're Filing Now

Document everything while you wait. Dates, photos, messages — a hearing 6-12 months out is decided on whatever record you've kept, not what you remember on the day.

A wait this long is a mismatch for time-sensitive issues. If the dispute is purely about money and under the small claims limit, that route can resolve faster than an LTB hearing — see our small claims court guide for Ontario's limit and filing steps.

If the delay itself is the problem — not just the underlying dispute — your MPP's office can inquire into a stalled file on your behalf. Our find your representative guide has the lookup tool.

Filing gives you a documented, timestamped record from day one — whatever happens with the LTB timeline.

Ways to avoid making the wait longer

You cannot shorten the Board's queue, but you can avoid delays of your own. File with the correct form the first time, since an application with a mistake can be sent back. Use the LTB's online filing where it is offered and keep your contact details current so a hearing notice is never missed.

Never ignore a notice of hearing: if you do not attend, the Board can decide without you. Keep a simple log of events and organise photos and messages in date order before the hearing, since you will be asked to summarise facts quickly. If both sides are open to it, mediation offered by the Board can resolve a dispute without a full hearing.

Hearing day basics

Read the notice carefully for the date, time and format of the hearing, since many are held by phone or video. Prepare copies of your evidence for the Board and the other side, and organise your story into a few clear points you can say in a minute.
