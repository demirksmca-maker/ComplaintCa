Canadians Lost $704 Million to Fraud in 2025 — the Worst Year on Record

Published September 11, 2026 · 3 min read
$704M
lost to fraud in Canada in 2025, according to the Canadian Anti-Fraud Centre — and that's only what got reported.

If online shopping fraud, phishing, or an impersonation scam is why you're here, the national numbers put you in very large company. The bigger problem is that most people in that company never file a report at all.

What's Actually Happening

The Canadian Anti-Fraud Centre (CAFC) recorded $704 million in reported fraud losses in 2025, up from $643 million in 2024 — a roughly 300% increase since 2020. Identity fraud, investment fraud, and service fraud (which covers most online-shopping and subscription scams) were the most frequently reported types. The CAFC itself estimates that fewer than 1 in 10 frauds are ever reported, meaning the real national loss figure is almost certainly several times higher.

Why So Few People Report It

Two reasons come up constantly: people don't know reporting is separate from getting their money back, and they don't know the right order to do things in. Reporting to the CAFC doesn't recover funds by itself — but skipping it means the pattern behind a scam (the account, the phone number, the fake storefront) never gets flagged for anyone else.

The Reporting Order That Actually Works

1Contact your bank or card issuer immediately if money already moved — this is time-sensitive, especially for a credit card chargeback.

2Report to the Canadian Anti-Fraud Centre (1-888-495-8501) — this documents the pattern even though it won't recover funds directly.

3File with local police if you have specific loss details — required for some insurance claims and larger cases.
