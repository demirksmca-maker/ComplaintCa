If your bank complaint has stalled, the landscape you're dealing with actually changed underneath you this year — and mostly for the better.

Quick steps

  1. Complain to your bank in writing and ask for its final response. Banks and federal credit unions have up to 56 days.
  2. If you disagree with the final answer, or 56 days pass, contact the Ombudsman for Banking Services and Investments (OBSI): 1-888-451-4519 or obsi.ca. Do it within 180 days of the bank's final response.
  3. Send your file: the bank's final response, a one-page timeline and your proof (list below).
  4. OBSI investigates. It must give its written conclusion within 120 days of having all the information it needs; most banking cases close sooner.
  5. If the complaint has merit, OBSI recommends compensation, up to $350,000.

October 2026 Update

An independent review of OBSI, released July 22, 2026, was largely favourable, but it found the sharp rise in banking complaints is putting real pressure on how fast OBSI can finish files. In 2025 OBSI opened 5,438 banking complaints, up 113%. OBSI's Early Resolution process, piloted from November 1, 2025, now runs on an ongoing basis and pushes banks to settle with you earlier. Expect investigations to take longer than the ranges below during busy periods.

What's Actually Happening

In 2025, the Ombudsman for Banking Services and Investments (OBSI) became the single external complaints body (ECB) for all of Canada's banks, ending nearly two decades where banks could choose between competing dispute-resolution services. Total consumer inquiries rose to more than 26,000 — up almost 60% from the record set the year before — and OBSI opened over 6,100 investigations, a 90% jump. Banking case volumes more than doubled year over year.

What It Means If You're Filing Now

One ombudsman means one process regardless of which bank you're dealing with — no more checking which dispute body your specific bank happened to use. In 2025, more than 1,300 complaints ended with monetary compensation totalling about $5.8 million, with resolution rates in the 40–55% range in favour of consumers and median compensation between $500 and $3,000.

Frequently Asked

How do I contact OBSI?

Call OBSI toll-free at 1-888-451-4519, or 416-287-2877 in the Toronto area. You can also email ombudsman@obsi.ca or start a complaint online at obsi.ca. Have your bank's final response letter ready.

Is OBSI free?

Yes. OBSI is free for consumers and independent of the banks. You must first finish your bank's own complaint process, which can take up to 56 days.

How long does an OBSI investigation take?

Typically 3 to 6 months from start to recommendation, after your bank's own complaint process of up to 56 days. Expect longer during busy periods.

Can OBSI force my bank to pay me?

No. OBSI makes recommendations rather than binding orders. Its recommendation limit has been published as $350,000 for banking and investment complaints. If the bank rejects a recommendation, you can still consider small claims court.

What do I need before contacting OBSI?

The bank's final response (OBSI cannot open a file without it), a one-page timeline with dates and amounts, supporting statements and screenshots, and a specific figure for the compensation you want with a short explanation.

How many complaints end in compensation?

In 2025, more than 1,300 complaints ended with monetary compensation totalling about $5.8 million. Median compensation was between $500 and $3,000.

Preparing a file OBSI can work with

A complaint moves faster when the investigator can understand it in one reading. Before you contact OBSI, assemble four things.

  1. A one-page timeline with dates, amounts and the names of bank staff you dealt with.
  2. The bank's final response, since OBSI cannot open a file without it.
  3. Statements, receipts and screenshots that support each point in the timeline.
  4. A specific figure for the compensation you are asking for, with a short explanation of how you reached it.

Remember that OBSI makes recommendations rather than binding orders, and that its recommendation limit has been published as $350,000 for banking and investment complaints; confirm the current limit on OBSI's site. If the bank rejects a recommendation, you can still consider small claims court.

Questions OBSI cannot answer

OBSI looks at fairness in how a bank treated you. It does not set interest rates or fees in general, and it cannot order a bank to change its policies. If your complaint is about a bank's pricing rather than how you were treated, explain the specific unfair outcome you experienced.

What OBSI can and cannot do: OBSI can investigate and recommend compensation up to $350,000, but it cannot force your bank to pay, fine the bank, or usually take a case that is already before the courts; if a bank refuses a recommendation, OBSI can make that refusal public.
