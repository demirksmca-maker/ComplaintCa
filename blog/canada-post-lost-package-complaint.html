Canada Post Lost Package: How to File a Complaint and Appeal

Published November 2, 2026 · 5 min read
By Kasım Demir · Founder, ComplaintCA

Short answer: call Canada Post, get a service ticket number, let them finish the investigation, and only then appeal to the Canada Post Ombudsman (1-800-204-4198). Skipping the ticket is the most common reason an appeal goes nowhere. The steps below follow that order and say what to have in hand at each one.

What should I do first if my parcel is lost?

Before you complain, rule out the simple causes. A parcel is not lost the day after the estimated delivery date. Check that the date has passed, that the address on the label is exactly right (unit number, postal code) and whether Canada Post has posted a service alert for your area.

If you bought from a store or seller, tell them at the same time. Many sellers will reship or refund while the parcel is being traced, and that is often quicker than waiting for Canada Post. Do not wait for one process to finish before starting the other.

How do I open a service ticket?

Call customer service at 1-866-607-6301 or use the support page on canadapost.ca. Have the tracking number, the ship date, the sender and receiver addresses and the service used. Ask for the ticket number and write down the date, time and name of the person you spoke to.

A short, factual opening works best:

“I am calling about parcel [tracking number], shipped on [date] to [address]. Tracking has shown no movement since [date]. Please open an investigation and give me a service ticket number.”

Why does the ticket have to be closed first?

The Ombudsman is the second stage, not a shortcut. Canada Post must investigate and close your service ticket before the Ombudsman will take an appeal. If you disagree with how it was closed (parcel declared lost, claim refused, or no useful answer), that is the moment to appeal. If you lost your ticket number, call customer service and ask for it again.

How do I appeal to the Canada Post Ombudsman?

  1. Have the closed service ticket number, the tracking number and your receipts or proof of value ready.
  2. Use the online appeal form on the Ombudsman’s page at canadapost-postescanada.ca/ombudsman, or call 1-800-204-4198.
  3. Explain what happened in order, what you asked for (refund, compensation, an explanation) and why the ticket outcome was not enough.
  4. Expect a follow-up within about 5 to 10 business days.

Two rules to know: the sender is the one who can file, so if you are the receiver, ask the sender to submit it. And the Ombudsman’s mandate document gives 12 months to appeal and does not review matters that are already before the courts.

What evidence should I keep?

  • Tracking number and screenshots of the tracking history, with dates
  • Receipt or invoice that proves the value of the contents
  • Ticket number, plus the date and time of every call
  • Photos of the packaging and the item, if it arrived damaged
  • Copies of messages with the seller or the sender

Frequently Asked

Who can appeal to the Canada Post Ombudsman?

Only the sender can file. If you are the receiver, ask the sender to submit the appeal.

How long do I have to appeal?

The Ombudsman’s mandate document says 12 months. Appeal as soon as your service ticket is closed, because evidence gets harder to find with time.

Do I need a ticket number to appeal?

Yes. Canada Post must have investigated and closed your service ticket before the Ombudsman will review it. Call 1-866-607-6301 if you need the number again.

What is the Canada Post Ombudsman phone number?

1-800-204-4198. You can also use the online appeal form on the Ombudsman’s page.
