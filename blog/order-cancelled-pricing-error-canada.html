Store Cancelled Your Order Over a Pricing Error? Your Rights in Canada

Published November 9, 2026 · Updated November 9, 2026 · 3 min read

Short answer: outside Quebec, a store can often cancel an online order placed at a mistaken price, as long as its terms allow it and you get a full refund. Quebec is different: its Consumer Protection Act bars a merchant from charging more than the displayed price, so you have a stronger claim, unless the price was so low you should have known it was a mistake.

You grabbed a TV at a third of its usual price, got a confirmation email, and then a "we're sorry, your order has been cancelled" message. Whether you can push back depends on two things: which province you live in, and how believable the price was.

Can the Store Cancel Your Order?

Outside Quebec, there is no consumer law that forces a store to sell at a mistaken online price. The answer comes from the store's terms of sale, which you agreed to at checkout. Many large retailers reserve the right to cancel orders with pricing errors, often until the item ships.

Check three things:

  1. Does the confirmation email say the order was accepted, or only received?
  2. Did the item ship or did the store charge your card in full?
  3. What do the terms of sale say about errors and cancellations?

If it shipped and the terms say nothing about errors, your position is stronger. If the store refunded you quickly and its terms cover errors, a complaint is unlikely to force the sale.

Quebec: The Displayed Price Usually Wins

Section 224 of Quebec's Consumer Protection Act bars a merchant from charging more than the price it displayed. Option consommateurs notes that Quebec is the only province where a consumer who buys at an erroneous price is entitled to receive the goods.

Courts have allowed one big exception: an obvious mistake. In the case of computers advertised at $2 each, the judge found the merchant had not meant to sell at that price. On the other hand, a court has also said a shopper may assume an online price was checked before it was shown to thousands of people.

Complaints go to the Office de la protection du consommateur at 1-888-672-2556.

What to Do Right Now

  1. Screenshot everything: the product page, the price, the order confirmation and the cancellation message.
  2. Read the terms of sale you accepted and note the clause the store relies on.
  3. Write to the store. Ask it to honour the order. In Quebec, mention section 224 of the Consumer Protection Act.
  4. Check your card statement. If the store kept your money after cancelling, ask for the refund in writing.

Frequently Asked

Can a store cancel my online order because of a pricing error in Canada?

Outside Quebec, often yes, if its terms of sale allow it and it refunds you in full. In Quebec, the Consumer Protection Act bars charging more than the displayed price, so the store usually has to honour it unless the error was obvious.

Does an order confirmation email mean the store must sell at that price?

Not always. Many confirmations only say the order was received. Check the email wording and the store's terms of sale, which often allow cancelling for errors until the item ships.

What is the rule in Quebec for a wrong advertised price?

Section 224 of the Quebec Consumer Protection Act bars a merchant from charging more than the price it displayed. Courts make an exception for obvious mistakes, such as computers advertised at $2 each.

Who do I complain to in Quebec?

The Office de la protection du consommateur, at 1-888-672-2556. Outside Quebec, contact your provincial consumer protection office.

The store cancelled but kept my money. What now?

Ask for the refund in writing with a deadline. If it does not arrive, contact your provincial consumer office and ask your card issuer about disputing the charge.
