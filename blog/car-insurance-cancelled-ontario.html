Car Insurance Cancelled in Ontario? Your Rights and What to Do Next

Published October 12, 2026 · Updated October 23, 2026 · 6 min read
$5,000
is the minimum fine for a first conviction of driving without insurance in Ontario, up to $25,000, under the Compulsory Automobile Insurance Act. Your licence can also be suspended for up to a year.

A letter from your insurer says your auto policy is ending. Insurers in Ontario can't drop you for just any reason, and the notice has to follow strict rules. Before the date in that letter, you have two jobs: challenge the decision if it's wrong, and make sure you are never on the road without coverage.

Step by Step

1Read the notice closely. Find the reason, the exact date and time the policy ends, and, if it's for a missed payment, the amount owed. Notices can now arrive by registered mail, courier, in person or by email if you agreed to electronic delivery. If an email looks off, check it with our Verify a Document tool before you click anything.

2If it's a missed payment, call right away. Ask your insurer or broker exactly what you must pay, and by when, to keep the policy. Get the answer in writing.

3Ask for the reason in writing. Under sections 237 and 238 of Ontario's Insurance Act, an insurer can only refuse, cancel or not renew an auto policy on grounds it has filed with the regulator, FSRA, and never on prohibited grounds. Ask which filed rule it is relying on.

4Complain to the insurer first. Every insurer has a complaint officer. Put your complaint in writing and keep a copy. Our complaint form can start the letter for you.

5Escalate if it isn't fixed. The General Insurance OmbudService (GIO) reviews home and auto disputes for free, and FSRA takes complaints about insurers that break the rules.

Getting Insured Again After a Cancellation

If your policy expired and wasn't renewed

Ontario has no grace period for auto insurance. If the policy expired and no renewal was paid, you are not covered, even one day late. Don't drive the car. Call your insurer or broker the same day: some will reinstate or renew if you act fast, otherwise you need a new policy before you drive again.

If you were cancelled or refused

New insurers will ask whether a policy was cancelled in the past few years. Tell the truth: hiding it is misrepresentation and can void your new policy right when you need to make a claim. A cancellation for non-payment can mean higher rates for a few years. A licensed broker can shop several companies at once, and if every company says no, the broker can place you with the Facility Association.

If your licence was suspended

Tell your insurer about the suspension. Don't drive while suspended, even with insurance. Once your licence is reinstated, expect insurers to treat you as a higher risk. Get quotes through a broker before your reinstatement date so you are covered the day you can drive again.

If Your Claim Was Denied Instead

A cancelled policy and a denied claim follow different paths. If the insurer refused to pay a claim, see Insurance Claim Denied in Canada? 4 Steps to Dispute It.

Frequently Asked

Can my insurance company cancel my car insurance in Ontario?

Yes, but only on grounds it has filed with and had approved by FSRA, Ontario's insurance regulator, and never on grounds the regulations prohibit. It must also give you written notice before the policy ends.

What should I do if I missed a car insurance payment?

Call your insurer or broker as soon as you get the notice. Ask exactly what you must pay, and by when, to keep the policy, and get the answer in writing. Don't drive after the end date if the policy lapses.

Who do I complain to about my auto insurer in Ontario?

Start with your insurer's complaint officer, in writing. If that doesn't fix it, the General Insurance OmbudService at 1-877-225-0446 offers free help, and FSRA at 1-800-668-0128 takes complaints about insurers that break the rules.

What if no insurance company will cover me?

The Facility Association is Ontario's insurer of last resort for drivers who can't get coverage in the regular market. Ask a licensed insurance broker to place you with it.

Is there a grace period if my car insurance expired in Ontario?

No. Once the policy expires without renewal, you are not covered. Don't drive until your insurer or broker confirms in writing that you have a renewed or new policy.

Do I have to tell a new insurer my policy was cancelled?

Yes. If a new insurer asks about past cancellations, answer honestly. Hiding it is misrepresentation and can let the insurer void your policy or deny a claim later.

Is there a fee to cancel my own car insurance in Ontario?

Often, yes. If you cancel before renewal, many insurers keep a short-rate penalty instead of refunding the full unused premium, so you get back less than a straight pro-rated amount. Ask your insurer or broker for the exact charge in writing, and have your new policy start the same day the old one ends.

Can my insurer cancel my policy without notice?

No. An Ontario auto insurer must send you a written cancellation notice before the policy ends, delivered in a way the rules allow. If you never got a notice, ask the insurer for proof of delivery and complain to its complaint officer, then the GIO or FSRA.

What happens if you are refused car insurance in Ontario?

Ask the insurer to tell you why in writing. Then use a licensed broker to shop several companies at once. If every company says no, the broker can place you with the Facility Association, the insurer of last resort, usually at a higher price. Don't drive without insurance in the meantime: it's an offence with large fines. If you think the refusal was unfair, complain to the insurer's complaint officer, then the GIO or FSRA.
