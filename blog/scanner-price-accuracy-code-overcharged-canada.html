Overcharged at Checkout? How the Scanner Price Accuracy Code Gets You $10 Off

Published November 9, 2026 · Updated November 9, 2026 · 3 min read

Short answer: at a store that follows the voluntary Scanner Price Accuracy Code, if an item scans higher than its shelf or advertised price, you get it free when the correct price is $10 or less, or $10 off when it costs more. In Quebec a law applies instead, and since May 7, 2025 the free-item limit there is $15.

$10
the most you get off a single item under the voluntary code outside Quebec. Items priced at $10 or less are free.

Busy holiday shelves mean more wrong tags and more scanning mistakes. Most big grocery, pharmacy and big-box chains have signed on to a code that turns those mistakes into a small win for you, but only if you speak up at the till.

How the Scanner Code Works

The Scanner Price Accuracy Code is a voluntary promise run by the Retail Council of Canada and endorsed by the Competition Bureau. When the price that scans is higher than the shelf label or the advertised price:

  • Correct price $10 or less: the item is free.
  • Correct price over $10: you get $10 off the correct price.

The free item or discount applies to one unit. If you bought several of the same item, the rest are sold at the lower price.

Some summaries describe the free-item line as "under $10" rather than "$10 or less". If your item costs exactly $10, ask the store which wording it follows.

How to Claim It at the Till

  1. Watch the screen as items scan, or check your receipt before you leave.
  2. Tell the cashier the item rang up higher than the shelf price, and ask for the Scanner Price Accuracy Code.
  3. At self-checkout, call the attendant rather than paying and leaving.
  4. If the cashier is unsure, ask for a supervisor or the manager on duty.
  5. Snap a photo of the shelf label if you can. It settles disputes quickly.

What the Code Does Not Cover

Government-regulated products such as alcohol and tobacco. You pay the lower price, but they cannot be given free.

Tampered barcodes or shelf labels.

Broken, malfunctioning or dead electronic shelf labels.

Stores that never signed on. The code is voluntary, so ask whether the store takes part.

If the Store Says No

Ask the manager in writing, with the date, store address, item and both prices. If that fails, complain to the Retail Council of Canada through its Scanner Price Accuracy Code page.

In Quebec, the rule is law, not a voluntary code, so complaints go to the Office de la protection du consommateur (1-888-672-2556). Two prices on one item? The Competition Act's double-ticketing rule says you pay the lower one, and the Competition Bureau (1-800-348-5358) takes those reports.

Store refused the scanner code? Turn the receipt and shelf photo into a short complaint.

Quebec vs the Rest of Canada

WhereThe rule
QuebecA law (price accuracy policy). Free if $15 or less, since May 7, 2025. Complaints: Office de la protection du consommateur.
Rest of CanadaA voluntary code. Free if $10 or less, otherwise $10 off. Complaints: Retail Council of Canada.

Frequently Asked

What happens if an item scans higher than the shelf price in Canada?

At stores that follow the Scanner Price Accuracy Code, you get the item free if the correct price is $10 or less, or $10 off the correct price if it costs more. The rule applies to one unit.

Is the scanner rule $10 or $15?

Outside Quebec, the voluntary code uses $10. In Quebec, the legal price accuracy policy raised the free-item limit to $15 on May 7, 2025.

Does the Scanner Price Accuracy Code cover alcohol?

No. Government-regulated products such as alcohol and tobacco cannot be given free, though you should still pay the lower price.

Do all stores have to follow the scanner code?

No. It is voluntary outside Quebec, but most major grocery chains, pharmacies and big-box retailers take part. In Quebec, the rule is required by law.

Who do I complain to if a store refuses?

Ask the manager first. Then complain to the Retail Council of Canada, or in Quebec to the Office de la protection du consommateur at 1-888-672-2556.
