Complaint About a Long-Term Care Home in Ontario? Who to Call

Published November 3, 2026 · Updated November 3, 2026 · 3 min read

Short answer: call the Ministry of Long-Term Care ActionLine at 1-866-434-0144. You can also take it to Ontario's Patient Ombudsman at 1-888-321-0339, which reviews long-term care homes too.

In This Guide

The ministry ActionLine

The Ministry of Long-Term Care takes complaints about long-term care homes on its ActionLine: 1-866-434-0144. The ministry describes its complaint process on ontario.ca.

Have the home's name, the resident's name, dates, and what happened ready before you call.

The Patient Ombudsman

Ontario's Patient Ombudsman reviews hospitals, long-term care homes, home care and community surgical and diagnostic centres. Phone: 1-888-321-0339.

Keep copies of any letters you sent the home. They show what you already tried.

Put it in writing

  • Who and where. Resident, home, unit or room, dates.
  • What happened. Short facts, in order, with staff roles if you know them.
  • What you want. One clear request.
  • Proof. Photos, notes, emails and care documents you are allowed to keep.

If it is about a doctor or nurse

A complaint about a doctor's conduct goes to the CPSO: how to file. A complaint about a nurse goes to the College of Nurses of Ontario.

Frequently Asked

Where do I complain about a long-term care home in Ontario?

Call the Ministry of Long-Term Care ActionLine at 1-866-434-0144, or contact Ontario's Patient Ombudsman at 1-888-321-0339.

Does the Patient Ombudsman cover long-term care homes?

Yes. It reviews hospitals, long-term care homes, home care and community surgical and diagnostic centres.

Can a family member complain for a resident?

Yes, family or a support person can usually raise a complaint on a resident's behalf. Have the resident's consent where possible.

Where does a complaint about a nurse go?

The College of Nurses of Ontario. A complaint about a doctor goes to the CPSO.
