Online Order Late in Ontario? The 30-Day Cancel Rule and Your Refund

Published November 9, 2026 · Updated November 9, 2026 · 3 min read
By Kasım Demir · Founder, ComplaintCA

Short answer: in Ontario, if an online order is not delivered within 30 days of the delivery date in your agreement, you can cancel it in writing and ask for your money back. The seller has 15 days to refund you. If it does not and you paid by credit card, you can ask your card issuer to reverse the charge.

30 + 15
30 days late lets you cancel. 15 days is how long the seller then has to refund you, before your credit card issuer can be asked to step in.

Black Friday orders often slip into December. A short delay is annoying; a month-long one gives you legal options in Ontario under the Consumer Protection Act, 2002. The trick is to use them in the right order, and in writing.

When You Can Cancel a Late Order

Ontario's online shopping rules give you the right to cancel if the product is not delivered within 30 days of the delivery date in your agreement. If no date was given, the 30 days run from the date you made the agreement.

Careful: you lose this right if you agree to accept the late delivery. If the seller emails "can we ship next week?" and you want out, do not say yes.

Cancel in Writing (Sample Message)

Email is fine. Keep a copy and note the date you sent it. A short message works best:

"Order #[number], placed on [date], was due by [promised date] and has not been delivered more than 30 days later. Under Ontario's Consumer Protection Act, 2002, I am cancelling this order and request a full refund within 15 days."

No Refund in 15 Days? Ask Your Card Issuer

If you paid by credit card and the seller misses the 15-day refund window, Ontario's Act lets you ask the card issuer, in writing, to cancel or reverse the charge and any interest on it. Send the request soon after the 15 days end, with a copy of your cancellation email; the law sets a deadline for the request.

The issuer must acknowledge the request and then either reverse the charge or explain in writing why it will not.

Paid by debit or e-Transfer? This reversal right covers credit cards. Call your bank right away anyway. If the issuer refuses, see what to do when a chargeback is denied.

Outside Ontario, and Other Next Steps

Other provinces have their own online purchase rules. Quebec and British Columbia both give buyers cancellation rights when goods are not delivered, so call your provincial office: Consumer Protection BC (1-888-564-9963) or Quebec's Office de la protection du consommateur (1-888-672-2556).

In Ontario, file with Consumer Protection Ontario at 1-800-889-9768 if the seller ignores you. For larger amounts, Small Claims Court is the final step.

Write a clear cancellation notice now, then reuse it for your card issuer if the refund never comes.

Frequently Asked

How late does an online order have to be before I can cancel in Ontario?

More than 30 days after the delivery date in your agreement. If no date was given, count 30 days from the date you placed the order. You lose the right if you agree to accept late delivery.

How long does an online seller have to refund me in Ontario?

15 days after you give notice of cancellation.

Can my credit card company reverse the charge?

Yes, in Ontario. If the seller does not refund you within 15 days, you can ask your credit card issuer in writing to cancel or reverse the charge and related interest. Send the request soon, as the law sets a deadline.

Does this work if I paid by debit or e-Transfer?

The Ontario reversal right covers credit card payments. If you paid another way, call your bank immediately and contact Consumer Protection Ontario at 1-800-889-9768.

Do I need to give a reason when I cancel?

You only need to state that the order was not delivered within 30 days of the promised date and that you are cancelling. Do it in writing and keep a copy.
