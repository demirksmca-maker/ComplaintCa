How to Dispute a Parking Ticket in Toronto: Screening, Hearing and the 15-Day Rule

Published October 8, 2026 · 5 min read
15 days
from the penalty notice date to pay or dispute a City of Toronto parking violation. After 60 days, there are no dispute options left.

Toronto parking tickets are no longer fought in traffic court. They go through the City's Administrative Penalty System: a written screening review first, then an independent hearing if you disagree. The deadlines are short, and one wrong move, like a partial payment, ends your dispute.

Step by Step

1Find the penalty notice date. Every deadline counts from that date, not from the day you found the ticket.

2Don't pay if you want to dispute. Once a full or partial payment is made, the City treats the matter as affirmed and resolved.

3Request a screening review within 15 days. Submit it online with a complete written explanation and your evidence, such as photos, receipts or a permit. You get only one screening review per violation.

4Late? Ask for an extension. With extenuating circumstances, you can request an extension for days 16 to 60. After day 30, the request must be made in person at North York Civic Centre or Metro Hall.

5Disagree with the screening? Request a hearing. Within 15 days of the screening officer's decision, request a hearing review through the online Administrative Penalty Lookup. The Administrative Penalty Tribunal hears it.

What the Screening Officer Can Do

DecisionWhat it means
AffirmThe penalty stands
ReduceYou pay less
More time to paySame amount, later deadline
CancelYou owe nothing

The City says relief such as more time to pay is available only if you show undue hardship. Write your explanation as if it is the only chance you get, because it usually is.

Expect a long wait. The City says the current turnaround time for screening reviews is up to 10 months. Your deadline is still 15 days, so file on time even if the answer will take months.

Write a clear screening review explanation with dates, evidence and what you are asking for.

Frequently Asked

How long do I have to dispute a parking ticket in Toronto?

15 days from the penalty notice date. If you have extenuating circumstances, you can ask for an extension for days 16 to 60, but requests after day 30 must be made in person. After 60 days there are no further dispute options.

Does paying a Toronto parking ticket end my dispute?

Yes. The City says that once a full or partial payment is made on a violation, the matter is considered affirmed and resolved. Don't pay anything if you plan to dispute.

What can a screening officer decide?

A screening officer can affirm the penalty, reduce it, give you more time to pay, or cancel it. You can request only one screening review per parking violation, so include your full written explanation and all supporting documents.

Can I appeal a screening decision?

Yes. Within 15 days of the screening officer's decision, you can request a hearing review through the City's online Administrative Penalty Lookup. A hearing officer of the Administrative Penalty Tribunal reviews the decision.
