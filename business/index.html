How to Collect a Debt From a Canadian Company (Guide for US Businesses)

Published November 6, 2026 · Updated November 6, 2026 · 3 min read

Short answer: check that the Canadian company is active and not bankrupt, send a demand letter, then file in the province where it is based. Small claims court is often the cheapest route if the amount is under that province's limit.

Most unpaid invoices between US and Canadian businesses are settled without a court. The ones that are not usually go wrong in the same places: a missed deadline, the wrong province, or chasing a company that is already insolvent. This guide walks through each step in order and links to a detailed page for each one.

1. Check the company first

1Find its legal name and status. Search Corporations Canada for federal companies. Companies incorporated in a province are not in that database, so search the provincial registry too. Use the exact legal name in every letter and claim.

2Check for bankruptcy. The federal Office of the Superintendent of Bankruptcy (OSB) has an online records search. It costs $8 per search and names the trustee if there is one.

3If it is insolvent, do not sue. File a proof of claim with the licensed insolvency trustee instead.

2. Watch the deadline

Most provinces give you 2 years to sue on an unpaid invoice; Québec gives 3. The clock usually starts when you knew, or should have known, the invoice would not be paid, and a written acknowledgment of the debt can move that date. The province-by-province deadlines are on our unpaid invoice page.

4. Choose where to sue

Sue in the province where the company is based. Small claims is the cheap option: Ontario takes claims up to $50,000 and Alberta up to $100,000, while Québec only lets small companies file. See the full limits table.

Above those limits you go to the province's superior court, where a lawyer is usually worth it. If your contract names a court or requires arbitration, that clause normally decides. See small claims for US businesses and, if you already won in a US court, enforcing a US judgment in Canada.

Frequently asked

Can a US company sue a Canadian company in Canada?

Yes. A US business can file in the province where the Canadian company is based, including small claims court if the amount is under that province's limit.

How long do I have to sue for an unpaid invoice?

Generally 2 years in Ontario, British Columbia and Alberta, and 3 years in Québec, usually counted from when you knew the invoice would not be paid.

How do I check if a Canadian company is bankrupt?

Use the Office of the Superintendent of Bankruptcy online records search. It costs $8 per search and shows the trustee handling the file.

Do I need a Canadian lawyer?

Not for small claims court. For large claims, superior court or enforcing a US judgment, a lawyer licensed in that province is usually worth it.
