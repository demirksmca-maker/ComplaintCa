How to Enforce a US Judgment in Canada

Published November 6, 2026 · Updated November 6, 2026 · 3 min read

Short answer: start a new court action in the province where the debtor has assets, asking it to recognize the US judgment. In Ontario this usually must start within 2 years of the judgment becoming final.

A US judgment does not work in Canada on its own. A Canadian court has to recognize it first; after that, it can be enforced like a local judgment. Courts there generally respect US judgments, but the deadlines are short.

How recognition works

1File where the assets are. Bring the action in each province where the debtor has bank accounts, property or customers who owe it money.

2The court checks jurisdiction. Canadian courts generally recognize a US judgment if the US court had a real and substantial connection to the case.

3Limited defences. The debtor can usually only argue fraud, unfair process (natural justice) or public policy. The court does not retry the case.

Do not miss the deadline

In Ontario, the Court of Appeal has held that the 2-year limitation period applies to enforcing a foreign judgment, usually counted from when the judgment became final (appeals finished or expired). Other provinces have their own rules, so check early.

Serve the Canadian company properly

When you sue in the US, serve the Canadian company in a way the Hague Service Convention allows; both countries are parties to it. A Canadian court may refuse a judgment where service did not follow the Convention.

After recognition: collect

Once recognized, the judgment can be enforced like a local one, for example by garnishing bank accounts or money owed to the debtor, or by seizing assets. If the assets are in more than one province, you may need steps in each one.

Frequently asked

Is a US judgment valid in Canada?

Not automatically. You must ask a Canadian court in the province where the debtor has assets to recognize it.

How long do I have to enforce a US judgment in Ontario?

Usually 2 years, generally counted from when the judgment became final. Missing it can end your claim.

Can the Canadian company reargue the case?

Generally no. Defences are limited to fraud, unfair process and public policy.

Do I need a Canadian lawyer?

Usually yes. Recognition is a full court action, and a lawyer licensed in that province can also handle enforcement.
