Canadian Client Won't Pay Your Invoice? What a US Business Can Do

Published November 6, 2026 · Updated November 6, 2026 · 3 min read

Short answer: gather your proof, check the company is active and not bankrupt, send a demand letter with a deadline, and if they still do not pay, file in their province before the deadline (2 years in most provinces, 3 in Québec).

An unpaid cross-border invoice feels harder than a local one, but the steps are the same: prove the debt, make a formal demand, then use the court or process that fits the amount. Doing them in order keeps costs low.

Gather your proof

1The agreement. A signed contract, purchase order, quote they accepted, or the emails where they agreed.

2The invoices. Invoice numbers, dates, amounts and the currency (USD or CAD).

3Delivery. Shipping records, sign-offs, timesheets or anything showing you did the work.

4Their promises. Any email where they admit the debt or promise to pay. Keep these; they can matter for the deadline.

Check the company

1Is it real and active? Search Corporations Canada for federal companies and the provincial registry for companies incorporated in a province. Note its exact legal name.

2Is it bankrupt? Search the Office of the Superintendent of Bankruptcy (OSB) records ($8 per search). If it is, file a proof of claim with the trustee instead of suing.

Frequently asked

What should I do first when a Canadian client won't pay?

Send a written demand letter with the amount, invoice numbers and a payment deadline, and keep proof that it was delivered.

Can I use small claims court as a US business?

Yes, if the amount is under the province's limit, such as $50,000 in Ontario or $100,000 in Alberta. In Québec a company can file only if it had 10 or fewer employees in the past 12 months.

What if the company went bankrupt?

You usually cannot sue. File a proof of claim with the licensed insolvency trustee named in the OSB records.

Should I invoice in USD or CAD?

Either works if the contract says so; state the currency clearly. Courts in Canada generally give judgments in Canadian dollars and convert a foreign amount, so what you recover can shift with the exchange rate.

Sources & Verification

Limits and deadlines come from provincial laws and court pages; the bankruptcy search details come from the federal OSB.

  • Corporations Canada: Search for a federal corporation
